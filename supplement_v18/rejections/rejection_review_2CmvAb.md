# Reject

The submission addresses a substantive part of GPU kernel optimization: evaluating dispatch decisions across shapes while accounting for correctness and tuning costs. Its explicit limitations, distinction between ranking and calibration, and reporting of unsuccessful hand kernels are valuable. However, the central probe-economics result contains a reversed break-even calculation, and correctness filtering is applied inconsistently to the performance comparators and reference-sensitivity analysis. These are scientific defects that the current qualifications do not resolve.

Independent recalculation from the supplied records reproduces the primary paired timing means and $t$ statistics, the gate's 53/56 eager deliveries, and the descriptive bandwidth residuals of 35.85% and 0.552%. These checks establish arithmetic agreement for those quantities, not validity of all their interpretations. I inspected the implementation and recalculated retained data; I did not reproduce GPU experiments.

1. **The reported revisit crossovers have the wrong sign.** Under the paper's accounting, let $P_b>0$ be the autotuning probe cost, $t_b$ its selected kernel time, and $t_g$ the gate's kernel time. After $v$ deliveries, the respective costs are

   $$
   T_b(v)=P_b+vt_b,\qquad T_g(v)=vt_g.
   $$

   A positive crossover therefore exists only when $t_b<t_g$, at

   $$
   v^*=\frac{P_b}{t_g-t_b}.
   $$

   In contrast, `m3_r42_analysis.py` retains rows with `a["t"] > t_g` and computes `a["probes"] / (a["t"] - t_g)`. These are precisely rows on which the tuned alternative is slower and cannot recover its positive probe cost. Consequently, the reported 122–628 revisit medians and finite-row counts do not establish the claimed amortization boundary. The convolution summary makes the contradiction especially clear: every budgeted arm selects eager torch on all 36 rows, while the gate selects torch on 35 and the oracle hand kernel on one. No budgeted convolution arm can catch this gate under the recorded accounting, including on the row assigned a finite crossover. Recompute both crossover values and the population admitting them; the reduction result cannot be recovered from the supplied aggregate summary alone. The one-delivery comparison is a separate result and is not disproved by this sign error.

2. **The claimed correctness-qualified delivery comparison includes incorrect compiled kernels, and several numerical claims use the superseded grid.** On the corrected 56-row union, `torch_cmp.pass_1e4` is false on five rows: four in m3m2 and one in w4. For example, the corrected w4 row $(N,H,C)=(3,5,32)$ fails. Nevertheless, `r42_analysis.py` includes all their timings in the paired delivery-regret statistics without consulting this flag. This does not measure delivery by two correctness-preserving alternatives. Report failed compiled cases explicitly and define either a validated fallback policy or a clearly conditional comparison on mutually passing cases; retain the failure accounting rather than silently dropping cases.

   The same corrected union contains **15/560** passing hand-arm checks against the default reference, rather than 20/560, and **80/280** passing IEEE checks against the fp32 reference, rather than 95/280. The fp32-reference rows with an admissible hand arm are **14/2/0** across m3m2/w4/a2, rather than 14/3/2. Thus the claimed a2 fp32 passing subset disappears after grid repair. Regenerate all associated counts and secondary comparisons from one canonical population. The held-out $t=1.64$ and $t=1.60$ results also support descriptive positive mean differences, not established superiority; the paper should keep that distinction explicit.

3. **The reference-sensitivity regret is not relative to a correctness-admissible oracle.** In `r41_gate_stability.py`, `gate_from_pass` always admits the original eager torch timing into the oracle and uses torch as an unconditional fallback, regardless of its pass flag under the replacement reference. In the retained rider records, torch fails the fp64-anchor check on **52/56** rows, and the reported gate delivers a failing arm on **31/56**. Under the IEEE reference, the corresponding counts are 35 and 24. Consequently, the stated $0$ to $+66.6$ percentage-point change is not a comparison of valid delivery regrets under the new contract.

   Recompute admissibility for every arm, including eager, and explicitly represent cases with no valid fallback. If a newly configured eager implementation is the fallback, its output and timing must be measured as that implementation. Use a consistent tolerance direction: the rider scales relative tolerance by reference magnitude, whereas the primary `allclose(ref, candidate)` check scales it by candidate magnitude. Moreover, this rider still contains the four obsolete shapes, so its 56-row population is different from the corrected delivery population. The stated torch flip fraction of 1.0 is also not the anchor flip fraction, which is 52/56; 1.0 appears in the bootstrap summary that the manuscript withdraws. These distinctions matter when reference instability is offered as a qualification of the central result.

4. **The demonstrated object is narrower than a frozen, zero-probe admission rule generalizing to unseen shapes and inputs.** The revised gate consults correctness verdicts measured on each evaluation row's particular tensors. Offline certification is a legitimate deployment assumption, but a passing check on one input does not certify a shape's input domain. The resulting experiment measures a structural rule augmented with evaluation-instance admissibility information. It does not independently validate a previously frozen admission filter across that domain. Either restrict the claim to these prechecked instances or freeze the admission policy using separate certification data and evaluate it on new inputs, with certification costs and restrictions stated separately. The warm accounting can remain useful, but it should not be described as measured cold-start economics.

5. **Essential evidence is not accessible as claimed.** The per-row `m3_r42.jsonl` ledger needed to correct the central economics analysis is omitted and assigned to an unspecified repository. The submission links the upstream benchmark, but supplies no accessible URL for that laboratory repository. The calibration-transfer summaries likewise omit the per-instance records needed to audit score-component and threshold analyses. In addition, the packaged scripts generally read `kernels/results/`, while the records are supplied in the sibling `results/` directory; several analysis dependencies are absent. Provide an immutable, accessible release with the required records and an explicit working execution layout. A content manifest does not substitute for the missing evidence. The reduction rerun should also fix and record the tensor-generation seed: its seed currently controls candidate ordering, while the reduction `torch.randn` allocation is unseeded in the runner.

The novelty is potentially an incremental empirical contribution, rather than a new general validation principle. The supplied rStation index contains no published same-problem papers. In the external literature, KernelBench [1] already jointly evaluates correctness and speed; DietCode [4] addresses shape-generic optimization and scheduling cost; Ansor [5, §7.4] distinguishes measurement budgets from tuning wall time; and Ovadia et al. [6] establish that calibration on one distribution need not survive a shift. These precedents do not establish duplication of the specific GB10 findings, but they place the novelty in the supported application and failure analysis, not in held-out testing or charged tuning alone. Reference [4] should identify the actual paper and its shape-generic search space and cost model more accurately.

Two components remain promising. The bandwidth result is reproducible as a descriptive lookup-repeatability observation, with the manuscript appropriately acknowledging that its coordinates encode total size and that a total-bytes baseline nearly matches it. The repaired guard's 32/48 accepted successes support a finite-population failure of its 0.90 precision target. Its identical scores across matched input-distribution strata provide a useful explanation for why a threshold offset alone cannot repair the constructed population. Preserve those scoped findings, while distinguishing that specific guard failure from general calibration-transfer impossibility. They do not rescue the materially incorrect central claims in this version.
