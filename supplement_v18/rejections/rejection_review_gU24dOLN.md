# Reject

The submission contains useful, task-relevant measurements of how reference arithmetic and numerical tolerances affect GPU-kernel evaluation on GB10. The profiler observations, precision-flag comparisons, and retained candidate-level records provide a substantive empirical basis. A contribution restricted to one device and library build can qualify for this problem. However, the current manuscript makes unsupported inferences from those measurements, mischaracterizes a close precedent, and gives incompatible descriptions of its prediction targets and selector policy. These affect the scientific contribution and its interpretation, rather than merely presentation.

My independent aggregation of the retained records reproduces all 672 admission labels at $10^{-4}$ and the admissible-arm counts $128,203,293,672$ across the four tolerances. I also reproduce the reported frontier identity counts under the producer's tie-breaking rule. These checks establish consistency of the retained numerical evidence; I inspected the relevant producers but did not reproduce the GPU experiments.

1. **The claimed novelty needs a materially more accurate comparison.** Sections 1.7 and 9.5 distinguish the tolerance-dependent frontier from Zhang et al. [10] by describing that work as a speedup study “under a fixed tolerance.” However, *KernelBench-Verified*, [Appendix J, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16241), explicitly evaluates tolerance sensitivity at $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-4}$, reporting both correctness and speedup and explaining selection effects from the changing admitted population. For example, its GPT-5.5 Level-1 results change from $99\%/0.90\times$ to $83\%/1.25\times$. Thus tolerance-dependent admissibility and performance summaries are already studied in the cited literature; tolerance is not an orthogonal variable overlooked there.

   The present per-shape minimum over a fixed candidate pool is a narrower object than that paper's aggregate analysis, and the GB10 dispatch map may supply additional empirical insight. Explain that distinction accurately and identify what the map and frontier teach beyond the established sensitivity. Du et al. [9] already establish reference/checker failures, while the Winograd analysis [7] and PyTorch documentation [8] establish relevant arithmetic mechanisms. The manuscript acknowledges much of this precedent, but its central novelty argument still depends on an inaccurate characterization of [10]. I do not conclude that every GB10 observation is duplicated; the claimed distinction is currently insufficiently supported.

2. **The frontier sweep does not establish the claimed exclusion of a class-based explanation.** Section 5.4 concludes, “No arm-class rule can produce that movement,” and says this rules out class dominance. In the retained ratio records, all five IEEE hand configurations have exactly the same candidate-scaled error ratio on each of the 56 shapes; the five TF32 configurations likewise have identical ratios within every shape. Consequently, a shape- and tolerance-conditioned precision-class admission rule reproduces all hand-kernel admissions, followed by selection using the recorded timings. The observations reject a fixed tolerance-independent admission rule; they do not rule out this class-based explanation or demonstrate distinct admission behavior among configurations within either hand precision class. The reported pairwise “dominance” counts also count admission of one class when the other has no admitted member, rather than direct speed comparisons. Make that meaning explicit.

   Frontier identity changes also need to be distinguished from speed improvements. The producer resolves equal median times by arm-name order. Of the reported $10/24/30$ identity changes, only $8/23/29$ have strictly lower recorded median time. At $10^{-2}$, for example, the `a2` shape $(N,H,C)=(2,6,48)$ switches from torch to an IEEE hand arm with exactly the same recorded median, approximately $0.028384$ ms. Some other changes have overlapping timing IQRs. This does not erase the broad tolerance effect, but it does invalidate reading every flip as a resolved performance change. Report tied winners and timing uncertainty alongside identity counts; IQR overlap or separation alone is not a significance test. Also keep relaxed-tolerance results explicitly separate from improvements satisfying the task's original fp32 contract.

3. **Reference-engine prediction and candidate-admission prediction are conflated.** Section 2.5 discusses the Winograd/TF32 dispatch partition and then claims that §4.6 predicts “this class decision” out of fold at AUC $0.9747$. That AUC concerns candidate pass/fail labels over 672 arm checks, not prediction of the reference engine. Section 4.5 similarly moves from engine-class predictability of approximately $0.604$ to an unprofiled “class decision” at AUC $0.9747$. These are different targets, populations, features, and metrics.

   Section 4.6 usefully acknowledges that the admission model's $96.4286\%$ accuracy matches the arm-only baseline, and reports the weaker within-hand result. Those qualifications must govern the surrounding conclusions. State separately what predicts reference dispatch, what predicts reference error given dispatch, and what predicts candidate admission. The admission AUC does not establish a deployable engine predictor or a calibrated within-class admission threshold. Likewise, two repeated observations at each of 17 cells support repeat consistency on that run, not the categorical “deterministic” dispatch claim in §1.4; §2.5 already recognizes the cached-decision alternative.

4. **The selector's executable meaning and reported costs are inconsistent.** Sections 6.3 and 6.5 specify policy A: retain the structural gate's choice if it passes, otherwise fall back to torch. Section 6.4 instead defines checked delivery as choosing the cheapest passing arm, which is policy B in the retained analysis. These policies select different arms on 11 of 24 reduction shapes. The fresh-run mean execution regret is approximately $4.0381\%$ for A and zero by construction for B relative to the same measured admissible minimum. Therefore §6.4 cannot describe its cheapest-passing rule and attach policy A's regret to it as “one policy.”

   Section 6.5 also says candidates are “timed only if” they pass, whereas `red_row` and `conv_row` in the supplied producer time each successfully executed candidate after computing its correctness result, without conditioning timing on admission. Excluding failed timings from a final ranking is compatible with this implementation, but it is a different protocol. Specify the actual policy, information available at selection, validation work, timing work, comparator, and cost exclusions consistently. Kernel medians alone do not measure the complete reference-building and comparison overhead.

5. **Clarity independently falls below the acceptance standard.** The reader must reconcile incompatible current statements across sections to determine what was established. The ambiguous “class decision,” conflicting selector definitions, and changing status of the certificate versus frontier as the central contribution are examples with scientific consequences. Substantial main-text space still follows repairs and experiment chronology: §6 opens with review history and a filter-inversion narrative, while §4.5 interleaves old and corrected model results. This obscures which protocol and evidence support the final claims.

   Organize the main text around one bounded research question, the final protocols and populations, the dispatch/error findings, and the tolerance-dependent performance result. Move superseded interpretations, audit histories, and repetitive experiment records into the supporting material. Define engine class, candidate precision class, admission, and frontier consistently. A per-shape speed-versus-error figure could make the contribution clearer while retaining numerical tables. These changes require resolving the scientific inconsistencies, not simply shortening the manuscript.
