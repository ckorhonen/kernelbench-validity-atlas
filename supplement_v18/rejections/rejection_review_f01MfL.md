# Reject

The GB10 measurements address a relevant problem: how reference implementation, precision settings, and dispatch affect correctness judgments for optimized kernels. The retained dispatch observations and composition counterexample could support a useful, explicitly bounded measurement paper. This version nevertheless has material defects in its primary certificate claims and in the new experiment intended to validate delivery under the benchmark contract.

The novelty is principally the measured GB10 dispatch/error map, not the general numerical principles. Barabasz et al. [7] already analyze Winograd error, conditioning, and accumulation order; PyTorch's documentation [8] explains shape-dependent numerical differences, TF32 defaults, and precision controls. DietCode [2] precedes the accounting of optimization cost across shapes. The manuscript largely acknowledges these distinctions. The supplied same-problem publication index contains no papers to compare against; the informal discussions cited as [5]–[6] do not establish publication precedence. I did not establish substantial publication overlap with the specific GB10 measurements. Novelty is therefore not the decisive reason for rejection.

I inspected the measurement and analysis code, including the missing kernel sources at the stated public commit, and independently recomputed selected results from retained records. I also independently refitted the corrected compression model. These are CPU record checks and source inspection, not reproduced GPU experiments. They confirm the six composition ratios, the union rule's four passes, and the corrected model's rank correlation. They also expose the following substantive problems.

1. **The primary certificate still conflates benchmark agreement with anchor accuracy.** Sections 4.1 and 8.4 correctly distinguish candidate/reference agreement, reference/fp64 distance, and a separately declared relaxed application contract. But §2.4 still calls zero `frac_over` “unconditional,” says no reference caveat is needed, and licenses sparse failures through tolerance loosening. Section 4.4 still says a speedup at `frac_over = 0.77` “is not a speedup,” while one at 0.005 is claimable on 99.5% of elements. Its schema defines delivery through `frac_over < 0.5`.

   These implications do not follow. A candidate identical to the recorded reference passes the benchmark regardless of that reference's distance from fp64. Conversely, two successive tolerance-sized discrepancies need not compose into a tolerance-sized discrepancy, as §4.1 itself recognizes. The fraction of reference elements close to an anchor does not establish which candidate elements are correct, an input-distribution guarantee, or whole-tensor correctness. The pinned evaluator [1] checks candidate/reference agreement over the whole output. Keep the diagnostic pair, but make actual candidate/reference validation the admission predicate everywhere. Any relaxed contract needs an explicit application justification and its own candidate-level evidence. This affects the contribution's meaning, not just terminology.

2. **The new held-out convolution experiment compares different operations.** In `kernels/s56_heldout_contract.py`, `conv_row` generates a random bias and computes `F.conv2d(x, w, b, ...)`. Its hand-kernel call receives only the input, weights, output, and dimensions. The pinned `lab/w3_conv_gemm.py` confirms that `conv_impgemm` stores the convolution accumulator without adding bias. The original `lab/w4_heldout.py` also uses a bias-free reference. Thus S56 does not reproduce the earlier held-out operation: it compares bias-free hand kernels with biased references.

   The resulting 0/4 hand passes and enormous budget ratios cannot establish that the original convolution deliveries fail for the numerical reasons discussed in the paper, or explain the earlier absolute-error screen as a “true negative.” Correct the operation mismatch and rerun this targeted comparison before drawing either a positive or negative delivery conclusion. The supplied records also give gate-hand budget ratios of approximately 17,347–28,839, not the manuscript's 17,431–23,783. S56 neither sets nor records the input RNG seed, and its determinism check repeats only the reference on newly drawn tensors, rather than checking the delivered hand computation as advertised. These omissions matter for reproducing the replacement evidence.

3. **The reduction's reported admissible-delivery cost includes an inadmissible delivery.** I reproduce the reported mean of 4.127% and SE of 1.401 percentage points over all 24 reduction rows. However, at $(K,M)=(393216,384)$, the selected `tsplit_S64_KT64` has budget ratio 1.2285 and fails. Its finite runtime regret, 1.9569%, is nevertheless included in that mean. Passing 11/12 hand deliveries does not make the selector contract-preserving on the remaining row; the preregistered 90% threshold cannot change the operation's correctness requirement.

   Report failure separately and evaluate a specified valid fallback, including any validation cost, before reporting a cost for admissible delivery over the whole grid. Merely excluding the failed row gives 4.222% over 23 rows, which is a conditional descriptive result, not a repaired all-row guarantee. Also distinguish the baselines: S56 times eager `torch.sum`/`F.conv2d`, and its admissible arm pool contains no `torch.compile` arm. This contradicts the manuscript-wide “torch.compile … never eager” statement. Finally, the numerical-error-versus-time-gap argument retained in §6.2 still cannot establish robustness of total-time rankings; the paper explicitly acknowledges that these quantities have no established mathematical link.

4. **The compression interpretation mixes different models and overstates the negative finding.** Section 4.5 combines the corrected engine classification's Spearman correlation, 0.986, with magnitude errors from the older classification. Refitting the corrected class-plus-geometry model to `a8_compress_v2.json` reproduces its retained predictions and yields median/worst multiplicative errors of **1.251×/2.409×**, not **2.09×/440×**. The latter values come from the older `a8_analysis.json` path used by `s55_review_repair.py`. Consequently, the stated evidence for making the magnitude field a lookup table is not the evidence for the headline model. Report rank and magnitude errors for the same clearly identified model, preserving the old model only as a separate comparison.

   Moreover, poor nearest-centroid classification does not show that the coordinates cannot predict dispatch. The qualification in §4.5 is undermined by §9.3's claim that the class is not a function of those coordinates and “no amount of fitting recovers it.” Likewise, 100% accuracy for the sampled binary label `frac_over > 0.5` does not validate whole-tensor admission or distinguish zero error exceedances from sparse failures. State the tested models' finite-grid results without turning their limitations into an impossibility result or a certificate for unmeasured shapes. A profiler pass identifies an engine; it does not itself measure candidate correctness or the reference's distance from fp64.

5. **The matmul observations do not establish the advertised depth threshold.** The code bisects $K$ while drawing shape-dependent random matrices and evaluating a maximum-error statistic. No monotonicity argument justifies that search. The retained data directly contradict the claim that this statistic is monotone: at $M=N=1024$, $\rho$ decreases from 1.232 at $K=3632$ to 1.009 at $K=3968$. The measurements at $K=3608$ and $3632$ establish two observed verdicts, not a unique crossing or a validity domain covering all $K\gtrsim3600$. Restrict the statement to those sampled inputs, or support a probabilistic transition with an appropriate neighborhood and seed study. Correcting the geometry scope alone does not resolve the input-dependence and monotonicity problems.

The composition evidence is more defensible when kept descriptive: the retained ratios are approximately 0.981, 1.507, 1.537, 1.000, 1.971, and 2.480, and c5 demonstrates that two individually passing stages can compose past budget. Six chains and a one-coordinate sensitivity probe do not establish a general propagation bound; the limitations already acknowledged should also govern the introductory recommendations to carry a worst-stage bound forward.

A revision need not add another architecture or an expensive broad experiment. It needs a consistent claim about what the certificate certifies, a valid replacement for S56, correctness-conditioned timing accounting, and model-matched evidence. The scoped dispatch measurements and concrete counterexamples should be the scientific core. The informative abstract should then be revised to reflect that scope, and references [5]–[6] should receive stable locators for attribution.
