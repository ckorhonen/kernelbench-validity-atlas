# Reject

The GB10 measurements contain a potentially useful contribution: profiler-observed convolution dispatch changes coincide with substantial changes in distance from an fp64 anchor, and precision controls affect the measured engine families differently. This is relevant to correctness-preserving GPU optimization. A carefully scoped study on one device can qualify; another architecture or a benchmark-wide campaign is not required. However, this version still makes unsupported explanatory claims, misattributes a measured selector difference, and presents incompatible accounts of its central contribution. These are substantive problems rather than minor presentation issues.

I inspected the relevant producers and independently recomputed quantities from the retained records. The 17 tuple cells have consistent engine labels across their two recorded repeats. The tolerance reconstruction agrees with all 672 recorded admission labels and reproduces the admitted-arm counts $128,203,293,672$, the frontier changes $10,24,30$, and their strict/tied splits. I also reproduced the two selector regrets and the 26 changed reduction admission labels. These checks establish consistency of retained evidence; I did not reproduce the GPU measurements.

**1. The dispatch measurements do not establish the claimed mechanism for the tolerance frontier.** Section 1.7 says that tolerance-dependent movement is produced by the dispatched reference engine, “which is why the frontier moves in steps.” Section 9.5 repeats this explanatory distinction from prior work. But a finite candidate pool produces a stepwise frontier whenever candidates have different admission thresholds, even with one fixed reference and no dispatch changes. With recorded candidate-scaled ratios $q_j$ and times $t_j$, the reported construction is simply

$$
T(\varepsilon)=\min_{j:q_j\leq \varepsilon/10^{-4}}t_j.
$$

Its stepwise form does not identify a dispatch mechanism. The acknowledged overlap of only two frontier shapes with Table 1 further prevents the dispatch map from explaining the frontier population empirically. The caveat in §9.5 is appropriate, but it does not make the stronger introduction claim supported. Either present these as separate descriptive measurements, or supply a matched comparison that holds candidates and inputs fixed while changing the reference realization and measures the resulting admissions/frontiers.

Relatedly, §§1.1 and 2.3 still describe reference error as setting how much tolerance budget a candidate has “left.” That allocation is not what the evaluator computes. If $e_c=C-R_{64}$ and $e_r=R-R_{64}$, agreement depends on $|e_c-e_r|$, not a subtraction of their error magnitudes from a shared budget. Section 4.1 correctly supplies counterexamples to the stronger interpretation. The central explanation must consistently preserve that distinction; an anchor diagnostic is informative without being a candidate-admission mechanism.

**2. Section 6.3 incorrectly attributes the entire regret change to changed correctness populations.** It explicitly says the approximately $0.54$ percentage-point gap is “not timing variance” but a correctness-population difference. Both labels and measured timings changed. Crossing the labels and timing tables from `s56_heldout_contract_recorded.json` and `s56_heldout_contract.json`, while applying the stated policy A and recomputing its admissible denominator, gives:

| Admission labels | Recorded timings | Fresh timings |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recorded | 3.49614% | 3.92334% |
| Fresh | 3.59633% | 4.03814% |

Holding labels fixed at the recorded values, replacing timings changes regret by $0.42720$ percentage points. Holding those fresh timings fixed, changing labels adds $0.11480$ points. Reversing the order also shows both effects. This is a crossed accounting calculation, not a new hardware experiment or an identification of why timings changed. It nevertheless directly refutes the manuscript's exclusive attribution. The 26 label flips support input-dependent admission, but do not establish that those flips explain the whole regret difference.

Keep the two observed runs, distinguish their inputs and timings, and withdraw the exclusive causal statement. Likewise, describe the probe comparison consistently as partial cost accounting: §6.5 lists validation work but supplies no measured comparison/reference-build cost, and warmed execution medians do not measure those costs. The scoped execution regrets are useful; a “total-time” conclusion excluding this work is not an evaluated deployment-cost result.

**3. The scientific argument remains materially inconsistent across sections.** This is an independent clarity concern. The reader can follow individual experiments, but cannot reliably identify which organizing claim survives:

- Section 1.1 introduces four measured quantities; §7.4 instead declares “The certificate is the contribution,” while §9 says the certificate is not a contribution on its own. The requirements for withdrawing a general certificate in §7.4 also do not follow from a device-specific measurement contribution.
- Section 2.4 calls the TF32 $1\times1$ row at $\rho\approx111$ “tail-conditional at every declared budget,” whereas §2.3 describes this family as a bulk reference failure with roughly 77–78% exceedance. These are incompatible interpretations under the stated diagnostic definitions.
- Section 2.5 says $\rho_{\max}$ compresses “as a bias statistic,” although §1.5 explicitly identifies whether it is a bias rather than a tail statistic as untested. A maximum normalized error and an out-of-fold rank correlation do not establish bias.

The main text repeatedly mixes current conclusions with earlier hypotheses, audit identifiers, review findings and retractions, despite assigning that history to Appendix A. Reorganize around the actual question, a single definition of each measured target, the experimental populations, and the supported results. Put superseded accounts and audit inventories in the supplement. This requires resolving conflicting scientific interpretations, not merely shortening sentences. The narrow dispatch study and the descriptive admission results should be understandable without reconstructing the research history.

**4. Correct the new normalization and the reproducibility claim.** Section 5.4's behavioral normalization is inconsistent with its own artifact. Summing `s60_pool_diversity.json`'s per-shape `error_sigs` gives **172**, not 168. Applying the script's rounding convention to admissible ratios gives **60, 75, 93, 172** across the four tolerances, not an endpoint progression of 32 to 168. Four shapes have a fourth signature, so multiplying the median of three by 56 loses observed distinctions. State the equivalence/rounding convention and recompute the counts; distinct maximum-error ratios also should not be equated with distinct output tensors.

The supplement's precision-specific convolution recorder imports `r1_conv_gated.py`, which is absent from the package. The advertised immutable revision `eca5275bca67d549e15ba3e8e60e951ffe53b1fe` did not resolve through the public repository during review: its raw source returned 404 and its commit lookup returned 422. The repository itself is accessible, and I could inspect the missing producer in its current tree, so this is not a claim that all evidence is inaccessible. Supply a resolvable revision containing the exact dependencies, or include them in the supplement. Current source availability does not substantiate the stated immutable reproduction path.

The focused literature comparison supports only a narrow novelty claim. Barabasz et al. [7] already analyze Winograd numerical error and conditioning; Du et al. [9] report official-reference deviations from fp64; and *KernelBench-Verified* [10], Appendix J/Table 6, already studies tolerance-dependent correctness and performance selection. I checked those sources directly. Section 1.7 also reverses [10]'s baseline change: that work enables TF32 rather than replacing a TF32 baseline with a stricter one. The supplied rStation publication index contains no papers to compare in this snapshot. The specific GB10 dispatch observations may add useful empirical detail beyond these precedents, but the general reference-dependence principle, tolerance sensitivity and stepwise finite-pool frontier are not new mechanisms.

A revision could retain the supported measurements without a larger experimental campaign. It needs a consistent, narrower contribution statement, correction of the selector attribution and behavioral counts, and a reproducible source pin. If the stronger dispatch-to-admission explanation is retained as the distinguishing contribution, it needs matched evidence rather than further qualifications around the present unmatched populations.
