# Reject

The submission addresses a relevant question for GPU kernel optimization: how reference arithmetic, numerical tolerances, and correctness checks affect which implementations can legitimately be selected. The GB10 convolution measurements, especially the different responses of Winograd and TF32 paths to precision controls, could support a useful, bounded empirical contribution. Rejection is warranted because the manuscript mischaracterizes a correctness-changing replication as timing variation, overstates the connection between its dispatch map and tolerance frontier, and retains materially conflicting explanations of its central claims.

Independent recomputation from the supplied records confirms the 672 candidate labels at $10^{-4}$, the admissible-arm counts $128,203,293,672$, and the frontier identity changes $10,24,30$. The strict-median/tie splits are also correct: $8/2$, $23/1$, and $29/1$. The simple arm-class baseline reproduces at $96.4286\%$. Policy A's reported reduction regrets reproduce as $3.49614\%$ and $4.03814\%$ for the recorded and fresh datasets, respectively. These checks validate arithmetic on retained records; they do not reproduce the GPU experiments or establish the manuscript's explanatory claims.

1. **The fresh selector experiment changes correctness, not just timing.** Sections 6.3–6.5 describe the fresh run as the same policy with a $0.54$ percentage-point difference attributable to timing variance, while repeatedly retaining the population description of nine admissible hand deliveries and three fallbacks. However, `s56_heldout_contract_recorded.json` has nine passing gate-selected hand kernels out of twelve, whereas `s56_heldout_contract.json` has twelve out of twelve. Its companion `s56_analysis.json` explicitly reports this latter result. The previously failing shapes change as follows:

   | Reduction shape $(K,M)$ | Recorded budget ratio | Fresh budget ratio |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | $(393216,384)$ | 1.60570 | 0.70596 |
   | $(1572864,192)$ | 2.57330 | 0.20596 |
   | $(1572864,384)$ | 7.11299 | 0.85895 |

   Across the reduction records, 26 per-arm correctness labels change. Consequently, both the admissible minimum defining regret and the policy's fallback decisions can change. The numerical regret calculations are correct, but attributing their difference solely to timing is unsupported. The fresh passing-hand mean is approximately $0.87078\%$ over twelve rows, rather than the recorded $0.86133\%$ over nine. Explain the input, implementation, or runtime differences between runs; provide their actual correctness populations and seed provenance; and separate timing variation from changes in admission and delivery. This is especially important for a paper whose main subject is the conditioning of correctness claims.

2. **The claimed mechanism connecting dispatch to frontier movement is not demonstrated on the frontier population.** Section 9.5 says the dispatch map explains the tolerance frontier and lets readers predict which shapes will move. Table 1 measures reference engine classes and anchor distances, whereas Section 5.4 recomputes candidate admission and selects minimum recorded times on a different population. Only two of the 56 frontier shapes exactly match Table 1's default-layout convolution tuples. The frontier records and their producer do not supply a matched engine-class analysis establishing the proposed explanation across that population.

   For a fixed candidate pool, expanding the tolerance necessarily expands admissibility; whether the fastest arm changes additionally depends on candidate errors and timings. An engine label or a reference-to-anchor distance does not determine those quantities, as Section 4.1's own counterexample correctly establishes. The measured flag interventions support an effect of dispatch/precision on reference error, but they do not by themselves establish which candidate frontier changes are explained or predicted by that effect. Supply a matched analysis or controlled reference intervention with candidates and inputs held fixed, or restrict the contribution to the separately demonstrated dispatch measurements and descriptive frontier. A complete causal or predictive study is not required for a useful partial advance, but its conclusions cannot be claimed without evidence.

3. **The total-cost conclusions exceed the correctness-conditioned accounting.** The post-validation execution regrets are well-defined, but Sections 6.1–6.3 still assert a total-time advantage over budgeted autotuning and argue that numerical perturbations cannot change the family verdict because timing gaps exceed numerical-error scales. These quantities have different units; acknowledging this does not justify the robustness conclusion. Admission can remove a fast candidate or change the fallback discontinuously.

   Section 6.5 excludes reference construction, correctness comparison, and other validation costs, and suggests approximating part of the check cost using steady-state execution medians. That does not measure the full cost of the executed policy, which also times every successfully executed candidate. Report a common correctness-conditioned cost model for the dispatcher and autotuner, including the actual validation and timing protocol, before drawing deployment or break-even conclusions. Alternatively, retain the explicitly limited execution-regret result and remove the unsupported total-cost comparison. Calling costs “charged separately” is insufficient when the relevant total is not supplied.

4. **The central argument remains materially unclear because incompatible claims coexist.** This is distinct from the correctness of individual measurements or the manuscript's length. Section 2.5 explicitly withdraws the assertion that the tuple experiment rules out a threshold, yet Section 4.5 still says it “rules out a threshold in the coordinates it varied.” Section 2.4 correctly says reference diagnostics do not decide candidate admission, then describes a row with $\rho_{\max}=111$ as “inadmissible” because of the reference-error distribution. Section 4.4 similarly mixes the diagnostic terminology with “bulk-inadmissible,” despite its stated separation of criteria. Section 4 labels certificates the primary contribution; Section 9 says the certificate is not a contribution on its own; the abstract instead centers the dispatch/frontier mechanism. These differences leave the reader unable to identify one consistent scientific conclusion.

   Reorganize the main text around a single stated contribution, with distinct definitions for reference accuracy, candidate agreement, engine prediction, and selection cost. Give each result its population and supported inference once. Move the extensive repair chronology, superseded interpretations, repeated audit declarations, and internal schema records into supporting material. Define experimental populations and candidate families in scientific terms rather than relying on project identifiers. The requested change is a coherent argument, not merely shorter prose or different headings.

The focused novelty check supports the manuscript's revised acknowledgment of prior work. Zhang et al. [10], *KernelBench-Verified*, Appendix J/Table 6, already studies tolerance-dependent correctness and speed summaries, including the cited $99\%/0.90\times$ versus $83\%/1.25\times$ example. Du et al. [9], whose retrieved title is *Measuring the Checker: Mutation Analysis for GPU-Kernel Benchmark Oracles*, reports reference-versus-fp64 failures. Barabasz et al. [7] analyzes Winograd numerical error and conditioning, and PyTorch's numerical-accuracy documentation [8] explains reduction-order differences and TF32 controls. Thus reference dependence, tolerance sensitivity, and algorithm-dependent rounding are established background. I did not find these sources establishing the submission's particular GB10 dispatch table; that bounded measurement remains a plausible distinction. The problem is the unsupported explanatory extension and inconsistent presentation, not an expectation that every measurement introduce a new numerical principle.

A revised paper should preserve the inspectable measurements and explicit limitations, resolve the changed correctness outcomes, and state only the mechanisms and costs actually demonstrated. The informative but densely packed abstract should then be rewritten to match that unified contribution. Correcting reference [9]'s title and minor notation inconsistencies would improve attribution and readability, but those minor issues are not reasons for this decision.
