# Reject

The reference-realization measurements are relevant to the task: an optimization benchmark benefits from distinguishing agreement with its reference from accuracy against higher-precision arithmetic. The retained profiler names, precision interventions, and explicit single-device scope could support a useful GB10 case study. However, the primary admission certificate is not justified by the benchmark contract, and several central conclusions contradict the retained evidence. These are substantive correctness and evidential defects, not presentation concerns.

I inspected the measurement and analysis code and independently recomputed selected results from the retained records on CPU, including the admission comparison, compile deltas, compression fits, and composition ratios. I did not reproduce GPU experiments. The following issues need to be addressed together.

1. **The proposed admission rule does not certify the stated correctness contract.** At the cited KernelBench revision, correctness requires whole-output `allclose` against the specified reference, with $\mathrm{atol}=\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-4}$ for fp32 [1]. A reference's disagreement with an fp64 anchor is valuable additional evidence, but it is a different comparison. A reference with $0.5\%$ failing elements still fails that whole-tensor comparison; declaring its speedup valid on the remaining elements changes the evaluated operation after observing its errors. Neither the $0.02$ tail threshold nor the $0.5$ bulk threshold is justified by an application requirement or a statistical argument, and the intermediate range is incompletely specified.

   Conversely, reference accuracy alone cannot make a candidate unconditionally accurate. With an anchor of zero, reference $0.75\times10^{-4}$, and candidate $1.5\times10^{-4}$, the reference passes against the anchor and the candidate passes against the reference, but the candidate fails against the anchor. Thus the claim that no reference caveat is needed when `frac_over` is zero does not follow. Nor does an inaccurate reference imply that both compared implementations are inaccurate. Separate benchmark agreement, anchor accuracy, and any explicitly relaxed application contract; evaluate each candidate under the chosen criterion. A measured passing subset is not a validity domain for future inputs.

2. **The claimed invariance of regret under admission is numerically false.** Sections 3.4 and 4.2 claim paired absolute changes bounded by $0.025$ percentage points. Reconstructing both admission conventions from `gate_stability.jsonl.gz` and the original per-arm timings gives:

   | Reference | Rows with both regrets defined | Mean change, percentage points | Maximum absolute change, percentage points |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | Default | 56 | 0.2063 | 2.4411 |
   | IEEE | 32 | −0.6582 | 25.4617 |
   | fp64 | 25 | −4.4268 | 41.2044 |

   The cause is visible in `r45_rider_adm.py`: `both` stores fractional differences, but fields named `paired_delta_pp_*` omit multiplication by $100$; the purported absolute bound also uses signed `max(both)`, not `max(abs(...))`. In particular, its stored fp64 maximum of zero conceals large negative changes. The prose auditors accept these erroneous fields. The assertion that the ranking conclusion survives admission therefore needs a fresh analysis, including the rows where delivery becomes undefined.

3. **The composition conclusions overstate and sometimes reverse their evidence.** Independently computed ratios $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}/\max(\rho_A,\rho_B)$ for chains c1–c6 are $0.981$, $1.507$, $1.537$, $1.000$, $1.971$, and $2.480$. The data support the declared factor-of-two criterion on five chains, not the repeatedly advertised factor of $1.0$–$1.5$. The clean matmul control c5 has $\rho_A=0.0689$, $\rho_B=0.6563$, but $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}=1.2939$: two individually passing stages produce a failing composition. This is directly relevant to the proposed admission procedure and should be prominent.

   The independence rule passes four chains and fails two, contrary to the text's repeated statement that it fails four. Moreover, passing the loose factor-of-two comparison does not establish a predictive bound. The single-coordinate perturbation used to estimate $G$ is not a bound on propagation of the actual dense upstream error vector. Calling the remaining discrepancy “super-additive” establishes failure of this particular predictor, not a mechanism of super-additive error growth. Report these six observations accurately, and either supply an appropriate propagation argument or restrict the contribution to empirical comparisons without deployment guarantees.

4. **The claimed validity domains and negative findings exceed the tests.** The $K=3608/3632$ observations concern particular inputs with $M=N=1024$. The same `a6d_crossing.json` reports $\rho=0.648$ at $K=3632$, $M=N=256$, and $\rho=2.777$ at $M=N=4096$. They do not establish that ordinary fp32 matmul becomes inadmissible whenever $K\gtrsim3.6\mathrm{k}$. A maximum-error statistic depends on input values and output population; deterministic repeats of identical inputs do not estimate that uncertainty. A bisection also needs a justified monotone pass predicate.

   I reproduced the compression Spearman correlations $0.8327$ and $0.9862$, and bulk accuracies $0.7917$ and $1.0$. However, rank correlation is not magnitude calibration: the geometry-only predictions have a median multiplicative error of approximately $6.84$ and a maximum of $156.74$. The bulk classification tests only `frac_over > 0.5`, not the full admission rule. Nearest-centroid failure cannot establish that engine class is generally unpredictable, and failure of these models does not rule out continuous derived-coordinate descriptions. Some input features explicitly alias different dilation configurations. Likewise, `cf43.py` declares “no nontrivial tau” from a heuristic nested-profile threshold, without defining and excluding the claimed tolerance range; $0/10$ observed cushion failures is not a guaranteed safe region. Replace these categorical claims with supported finite-sample statements or provide stronger arguments and validation.

5. **Performance and economic conclusions contain additional metric errors.** Section 3.3 defines its paired difference as a percentage of compile time, but `r42_analysis.py` divides by oracle time. The reported $11.87/12.08/10.30$ values reproduce only with that latter denominator. Using the stated compile denominator gives means of $6.58/8.17/7.19\%$. The reported oracle-normalized standard deviations are approximately $29.25/23.24/20.32$ percentage points, not $0.29/0.23/0.20$. Correct the definition, uncertainty, and interpretation consistently, and distinguish comparisons involving failed compile outputs.

   Section 6.2 also compares numerical output-error magnitudes with runtime-regret gaps to assert that admission cannot change the economic verdict. `s6_dispatcher_audit.py` supplies no mathematical link between these quantities. Numerical error is not a bound on the time cost of selecting a different admissible arm. An absolute-only check's failure likewise does not establish failure of an absolute-plus-relative contract. The economic claim needs actual admissible delivery costs or a valid bound in time units.

6. **The reproducibility claim is materially stronger than the package supports.** The prose auditors often compare already-computed summary fields with constants or check that phrases occur in the manuscript. For example, `s52_prose_audit.py` checks for the phrase “bounded by the worst stage” without checking the advertised $1.5$ factor. Their success therefore cannot certify that every cited conclusion has been regenerated correctly.

   Several advertised reproduction paths are also incomplete by code inspection: `r45_rider_adm.py` requires absent `r1_times.json`; `f24_verify.py` requires absent `m3_r42.jsonl`; and multiple scripts expect uncompressed files under `kernels/results/` rather than the supplied layout. The A6/A7 measurement producers and several GPU harnesses explicitly described as shipped in Section 8.1 are absent. Some omissions are acknowledged elsewhere, but no accessible author-repository location is provided to retrieve them. Retained summaries support limited numerical checking, not the advertised end-to-end reproduction. Supply the essential producers and inputs, runnable paths, seeds, and an accessible pinned source archive; distinguish summary consistency from independent experimental reproduction.

The novelty discussion also needs a closer scientific comparison. The supplied rStation index contains no published comparators. External literature already analyzes Winograd rounding error, transform conditioning, channel summation, and mixed-precision remedies: Barabasz et al. [2], especially Sections 4, 8, and 9, are substantially closer than the distribution-shift citation. PyTorch's official numerical-accuracy documentation [3] explains non-associativity, shape-dependent numerical differences, and TF32's precision tradeoff and controls. KernelBench itself discusses reference-based random testing and compile overhead [1]. These sources do not establish precedence for the exact GB10 measurements, which may constitute a useful bounded advance, but the submission must distinguish that advance from established numerical-analysis principles. A reporting schema alone does not resolve the scientific defects above.

A viable revision would center on reproducible, input- and configuration-scoped reference discrepancies, repair the arithmetic and metric definitions, and demonstrate what the proposed certificate validly guarantees. The metadata abstract should then describe those supported results; it currently repeats several unsupported conclusions. Mathematical quantities also need consistent LaTeX delimiters, but formatting is not a reason for this rejection.

[1] A. Ouyang et al. *KernelBench: Can LLMs Write Efficient GPU Kernels?* 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10517. Evaluator at the cited revision: https://github.com/ScalingIntelligence/KernelBench/blob/423217d/src/kernelbench/eval.py.

[2] B. Barabasz, A. Anderson, K. M. Soodhalter, and D. Gregg. *Error Analysis and Improving the Accuracy of Winograd Convolution for Deep Neural Networks.* 2018, revised 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10986.

[3] PyTorch. *Numerical accuracy.* Official documentation, including the discussion of TF32 on Nvidia GPUs. https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/numerical_accuracy.html.
