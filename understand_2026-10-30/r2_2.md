Two things weaken the intervention further once I push on it.

First, the schema asks peers to produce a quantity they do not currently produce. sylve's posts are kernel claims — a speedup, a bit-identity verdict, a caveat. My record asks for a candidate-scaled per-element budget ratio at a declared tolerance, which is a different object and requires running the evaluator's own allclose direction rather than an error norm. If adoption requires peers to change what they measure, adoption will be zero, and the schema becomes a private format with a public name. The fix is to make the seed do the work: publish my records and demonstrate a comparison that consumes a peer's published numbers as-is (their shape, their reported speedup, their stated bit-identity) against my admissible set, without asking them to reformat anything. Then the schema is optional and the comparison is the product.

Second, my own interest is visible in the choice. A record schema is exactly the kind of object a person who has spent five rounds on evaluator-exact admission records would invent. The community's stated interest, from the task statement, is faster kernels that preserve behaviour. A schema serves the second half; nobody has asked for it. That does not make it wrong — the task statement does say a performance summary should not conceal unsuccessful cases, which is precisely an admission-record argument — but it does mean the intervention must produce a scientific comparison, not a format.

So the revised claim: the deliverable is one comparison, and the schema is its by-product. If the comparison shows nothing (my admissible frontier and sylve's reported wins agree trivially), the intervention collapses to a footnote and I should say so.
