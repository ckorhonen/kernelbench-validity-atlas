# Reject

The paired reference intervention is relevant to reliable GPU-kernel evaluation and could constitute a useful, narrowly scoped contribution. The retained records support the baseline pass counts of 56/56, 21/56 and 4/56, the reported engine-label distribution, and zero disagreements between the two tolerance predicates across 2,016 cells. However, the manuscript does not consistently define the selection outcome carrying its central claim. Its headline selection counts implement a different policy from the one stated in the main text. Additional population and inference errors materially affect the scientific interpretation.

I independently recomputed counts from the retained row-level artifacts and inspected the relevant producers and KernelBench evaluator at revision `423217d`. These checks validate arithmetic and expose method/reporting mismatches; they are not independent GPU reproductions.

1. **The central selection experiment does not implement the stated delivery policy.** Section 2.3 defines the delivered arm as the fastest admitted arm, falling back to the baseline only when no arm is admitted. In both `r41_gate_stability.py` and `s63_engine_predicate.py`, `gate_from_pass` instead uses a fixed rule: when $H\leq14$ and $C\leq64$, try `imp_B32x32` in precision order; otherwise return `torch`. It does not minimize over admissible arms, and it can return a failing baseline when admissible alternatives exist. The reported 11/56 and 21/56 changes are changes under that fixed rule.

   This distinction changes the result substantially. Joining the retained candidate-scaled labels to `r1_times.json`, even allowing all recorded timing ties, the fastest-admissible choices with the stated empty-pool fallback have disjoint choice sets on 19 rows for default→tf32-off and 49 rows for default→fp64. For example, at `m3m2|4|1|256`, the anchor admits all five IEEE hand arms, but the recorded rule returns the failing `torch` arm because $C>64$. There are 27 anchor rows where the rule returns an inadmissible arm despite a nonempty admissible pool.

   The “delivery-status changes” of 35/56 and 52/56 also need a precise definition. Binary availability of at least one admissible arm changes on 20 and 4 rows, respectively. The recorded rule's selected-arm validity changes on 24 and 31 rows; its identity changes on 11 and 21 rows. Combining identity and validity is a possible outcome definition, but it is not binary delivery availability. State one policy and distinguish arm identity, selected-arm validity, nonempty-pool status and full-pool membership. Regenerate the headline, abstract, table and regret interpretation under those definitions. The manuscript cannot leave the reader to reconstruct its principal outcome from different conventions.

2. **Several central summaries contradict their own evidence.** Sections 1.2, 2.2 and 3.3 report membership changes of 15/56 and 48/56 without consistently identifying the hand-only pool, while §2.3 supersedes those with full-pool counts of 35/56 and 52/56. Both populations can be informative, but their names and denominators must remain explicit throughout, including the abstract.

   Section 3.3 additionally assigns 52 baseline flips, 49 compiled-baseline flips and 48 flips per IEEE tile to the engine-class swap. Those are the anchor-swap counts. The tf32-off swap has 35, 35 and 15, respectively, totaling the stated 145 individual flips. Section 3.5 says there are 168 class-cells while also reporting 172 admissible class-cells at the loosest tolerance; the retained error-signature counts total 172. These are not merely cosmetic discrepancies: they change which intervention and population support the conclusions. The abstract and main text need an integrated reconciliation rather than another assertion that the auditors pass.

3. **The input-draw result has the wrong population label and does not estimate an admission probability.** The two held-out artifacts contain 168 reduction arm comparisons and 60 convolution comparisons. All 26 flips are in reductions. Thus $26/228=11.4\%$ is the pooled discordance fraction across both families; the reduction-only fraction is $26/168\approx15.5\%$. Sections 2.6 and 3.4 and the abstract describe 228 as reduction arm checks, and §5 calls the input-draw axis the “same population” despite its difference from the 56 convolution shapes.

   Comparing two realizations demonstrates input sensitivity. It does not establish an arm's admission probability over an input distribution, nor make the observed fraction independent of the particular draws. The unrecorded seed in the earlier run further limits reproducibility. Report this as paired, two-run discordance with the correct denominators and explicit scope. A stronger admission-rate claim would need repeated draws and uncertainty accounting for shared shapes and correlated arms. Also say that the reference *configuration* is fixed: its output necessarily changes with the inputs.

4. **The main text overstates what the numerical and prediction studies establish.** Section 3.2 presents the $K=3608$ to $3632$ matmul crossing as a property of accumulation depth, without the qualification supplied by `a10_crossing_neighborhood.json`. That study reports pass fractions of 4/6 and 2/6 at those endpoints, zero monotone seeds, and no sharp transition. Its stated conclusion is to report a stochastic transition rather than a threshold. This directly relevant contrary evidence belongs beside the crossing claim in the main text.

   Likewise, Spearman correlations of 0.833 and 0.986 support rank prediction, not the “predictable in magnitude” claim in §3.2; §1.4 itself correctly limits the result to ordering. The weak engine classifier does not establish §4's assertion that the reference “is not pinnable,” or that its dispatch is intrinsically unpredictable from a complete configuration. Predictor inadequacy and run-to-run dispatch variability are different hypotheses, and fresh-process replication remains unperformed. Finally, disabling TF32 preserves tensor dtype but changes arithmetic precision as well as potentially changing algorithm family. Describe the observed intervention and engine transitions without implying an isolated algorithm effect at unchanged arithmetic precision. These claims can be narrowed without requiring an extensive new experiment.

5. **The reproducibility and comparator description needs correction.** The opening statement “torch.compile … throughout, never eager PyTorch” is false for the held-out delivery results. The inspected `s56_heldout_contract.py` explicitly contains an eager-only baseline and no compiled arm; the convolution selection producers also use eager `torch` as fallback. Label each result's actual comparator and oracle pool. The universal claim of interleaved A/B timing is also unsupported by `r1_conv_gated.py`, which times arms sequentially in separate 12-repetition calls. Synchronization, warm-up and spread records are useful, but they do not establish that ratios cannot drift within a run.

   The central GPU producers also write to `kernels/results/`, whereas the shipped artifacts reside in the sibling `results/` directory; `s63_engine_predicate.py` neither resolves that layout nor creates the output directory. Provide working reproduction commands and paths for the central experiment, with its actual dependencies. The retained margins are valuable inspectable evidence, but runnable auditors are not a substitute for a usable measurement producer.

The focused literature comparison supports a narrower novelty claim than “reference dependence” itself. Du et al. [9], *Measuring the Checker: Mutation Analysis for GPU-Kernel Benchmark Oracles*, already compare official references against fp64 and document violations of benchmark tolerance. Zhang et al. [10], *KernelBench-Verified*, already study TF32 baseline configuration and tolerance-dependent correctness and selection effects, including Appendix J. Barabasz et al. [7] establish the numerical-error mechanisms of Winograd convolution. The paired GB10 dispatch observations and their consequences for a precisely defined selector could add useful evidence beyond these works; the broader premise is already established. Correct [9]'s title and complete [11]'s bibliographic entry.

Clarity is a separate concern here. The research question is identifiable, but the decisive operational definitions are inconsistent, and readers must consult producer code and historical repairs to understand which selection result is claimed. A revised paper should organize the main argument around one explicit reference intervention, one defined candidate pool and policy, and one reconciled outcome table. The architecture restriction and absence of a universal speedup claim are appropriate limitations and are not reasons for rejection. The unresolved scientific and communication defects above are.
