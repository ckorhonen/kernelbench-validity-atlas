NEW REFLECTION — the assumption. The community's repeated assumption is that the reference implementation is
simultaneously the correctness oracle and the performance baseline: "passes against torch" and "faster than
torch" are treated as one comparison against ground truth. My own artifacts say this is an inherited belief,
not a proven necessity. In the rider redo (results/r45_rider_adm.json), torch fails the fp64 anchor on 52 of
56 rows (bootstrap flip median 1.0) and passes its own check on only 21/56 rows under the ieee realization and
4/56 under fp64. A rule that admits the baseline unconditionally is therefore certifying nothing about the
baseline while demanding the candidate prove itself — the asymmetry my third reviewer round caught (F3).

Credible alternative, already half-built here: treat the reference as a realization, not an oracle. Admit
every arm, baseline included, against a declared anchor lattice (R_def/R_ieee/R_64), and attach the
certificate to (rule, domain predicate, anchor). The consequence is measurable rather than rhetorical: 165 of
672 arm-anchor cells pass ONLY against fp64 (cf43.json), so a single-anchor verdict is a coin flip on
which anchor you happened to pick; the ieee↔64 exact agreement is 39.3%; and 0 of 560 realized verdict cells
change between candidate-scaled and reference-scaled tolerance direction, which tells me the direction debate
is hygiene while the anchor choice is substantive. Under enforced admission the dispatcher's delivered arm
changes on 52/56 rows (21 arm→arm, 31 arm→none) while its regret magnitudes move by at most 0.025 pp
(paired, r45) — the ranking survives, the delivery claim does not.

Evidence that would distinguish this from the prevailing view: (a) a benchmark where candidate and baseline
are scored under the same admissibility rule, showing that baseline failure rates are non-trivial (ours: 52/56
vs fp64) rather than a rounding curiosity; (b) a case where the same kernel flips delivery verdict across
anchors on identical timings — we have 169/528 reduction pass-flips at bars 1.1–1.8e-4 (cf43 CF3), which is
that case for tolerance rather than anchor, and the arm-anchor patterns are its anchor analogue; (c) an
independent reimplementation of the harness reaching the same flip counts. If a community's papers all report
speedups with baseline-as-oracle and no anchor declaration, their numbers are not comparable across labs even
when the arithmetic reproduces — which is exactly what happened in my own three repair rounds, where every
reviewer reproduced my arithmetic and rejected my interpretation.
