Building on round 1: two corrections to my own framing, then the design of the two measurements.

Correction 1 — the two measurements in round 1 were both framed as ''the paper''s problem''. They are
actually the program''s instrument problem, and it has a different owner. The certificate is a statement
about a recorded reference realization; the reference is produced by the benchmark harness (or by our
fp64 anchors), and the reference''s budget consumption is a property of the reference, not of the
candidate. So the right level of generality is: any benchmark that scores a candidate against a recorded
reference with a tolerance budget has exactly this three-place structure, and our paper''s claim is that
the third place is currently unreported. That reframes the two measurements as benchmarking-method
instruments rather than kernel measurements. A second architecture is interesting only insofar as its
reference menu exercises the same three-place structure differently; the input-distribution sweep is the
cleanest test of whether the diagnostic pair''s magnitude field is universal (a property of tolerance
budgets) or device-specific (a property of cudnn''s engines).

Correction 2 — I overstated the fragility of the certificate in round 1. The certificate is not ''fragile''
in the sense of needing precise numbers; it is exact. Benchmark agreement at a recorded realization is
binary; the diagnostic pair is what constrains the interpretation of any ratio. The failure mode was not
the certificate but the prose that called a diagnostic an admission predicate. The round-5/6 reviews did
not find a weak certificate; they found a mislabeled one. From now on the paper says ''a ratio between
arms that both miss the contract is a ratio with no anchor'' and nothing more about the candidate. That is
the whole repair, and it is cheap to maintain because the schema now records frac_over and rho_max
explicitly.

Design of the two measurements, now with kill conditions stated before execution:

Measurement A — anchor-lattice reproduction on a second architecture. Protocol: same 56-row conv union,
same three anchor realizations (default as configured, explicit ieee, fp64), same admission rule
(frac_over-keyed, three levels), same three criteria per row. Measurements: (A1) frac_over and rho_max for
every row at the second arch; (A2) baseline torch arm pass-own-check rate under default/ieee/fp64 (predicted
different from GB10''s 56/21/4, because the engine menu differs — that difference is the point); (A3) the
anchor-profile mix (pass-none / pass-only-X / all-three) over 672 cells. Decision rule: if the second arch
shows pass-none == 0 across all anchors (i.e., the reference is not budget-constrained anywhere) => the
frac_over diagnostic has no traction on that arch => the certificate is GB10-specific and §4.5''s
generalization claims are scoped accordingly. Kill condition recorded: ''a second arch shows baseline
admissibility trivially satisfied''. That kill is exactly the one the refresh prompt named as the
alternative branch: ''a second arch shows baseline admissibility trivially satisfied — then the two-class
decomposition is a GB10 artifact''.

Measurement B — input-distribution sweep at fixed shape, on the existing GB10 stack (no new hardware).
Protocol: the L1/40 LayerNorm row (A11) at six distributions (default U[0,1); kappa 100; kappa 1000 x
normal/randu/1000x-magnitude-shifted; constant rows as a degenerate control), same fp64 anchor, same
admission rule. Measurements: (B1) rho and frac_over per row per distribution; (B2) the candidate
split-statistics LayerNorm row''s rho and its verdict under each distribution (predicted: rho 0.663 at
kappa 1000, anchor-clean, but the candidate''s own verdict flips between admissible and inadmissible as
the reference''s budget consumption changes — this is the prediction channel the B5 stage-2 found:
family-constant z components preserve within-family ordering while shifting the absolute score); (B3) the
input-scaling axis: kappa = |mean|/std, predicted near-linear (exponent 0.85 fitted over 2.76 decades).
Decision rule: if the diagnostic pair fails to separate rows by whether the reference''s own budget
consumption is a mechanism vs an accident (i.e., a row with rho < 0.1 whose verdict flips) => kappa is not
the mechanism and the pair is a label, not a law. Kill: ''a family whose rho is below 0.1 yet whose
verdicts flip under anchor swap''.

Both measurements are pre-registered in the sense that the decision rules are written before the runs;
Measurement A needs a second arch (parked, it is the named kill) and Measurement B needs only the
existing stack. The program''s next concrete number that moves the paper is Measurement B''s row B2: does
the candidate''s verdict flip as the reference''s budget consumption crosses rho = 1? If yes, the
certificate''s admission rule is doing real work, and the paper''s flagship claim (admission is a
three-place relation) gains its first confirming experiment. If no, the admission rule is vacuous on the
LN family and the certificate''s scope is narrowed to the conv family where the two classes exist.
