# Reject

The submission contains a potentially useful, bounded measurement study: the GB10 convolution dispatch map, the large separation between Winograd and TF32 reference error, the anchor-dependent admissibility table, and the released row-level records are substantive observations. The supplied CPU-side recomputations reproduce the main arithmetic (including the 0/24/31 delivery disagreements, the 0.986/0.833 rank correlations, and the 0.974652 admission AUC). Those strengths do not resolve the problems below.

The central validity claim substantially overlaps prior work that is absent from the related-work discussion. *Measuring the Checker: Mutation Analysis for GPU-Kernel Benchmark Oracles* (Du et al., arXiv:2609.22220, published 2026-09-02) makes tolerance blindness and reference-versus-fp64 failures a central result, measures tolerance blind bands growing with reductions, and reports official references that violate their own KernelBench tolerance against fp64 across 48 architectures. *KernelBench-Verified* (Zhang et al., arXiv:2607.16241, 2026-06-26) directly studies TF32 reference baselines and correctness distributions, while Agarwal et al., *How Much of a Real Workload Can LLM-Generated GPU Kernels Actually Reach?* (arXiv:2609.21058, 2026-09-17) analyzes KernelBench tolerance failures and checker limitations. The paper instead says that the relevant principles are covered by KernelBench, Winograd analysis, and PyTorch documentation, and presents the reference/tolerance premise as if those were the nearest precedents. The GB10-specific dispatch measurements may still be a distinct contribution, but the manuscript does not establish that distinction or compare methods, populations, and conclusions against these earlier papers. Given that the reference-conditioned validity question is the paper's organizing contribution, this is a substantial novelty and attribution defect rather than a citation omission.

The current text is also materially difficult to read as a scientific paper. It is organized as a repair and review log: “round-8,” “round-11 review,” “round-13 review,” “the reviewer of submission Lo23js…,” “this tick,” retractions, P2 schema rows, and superseded values appear throughout the main argument. Section 1 promises that the review history and retraction ledger are in “Appendix A,” but no Appendix A is supplied. A reader must reconstruct which population is current from competing as-run and corrected results (for example, 52/56 versus 53/56 deliveries, and the several selector-regret populations) before understanding the scientific conclusion. The audit tables and provenance discussion are useful supplement material, but they displace the research question, method, and evidence in the main text. Reorganize the paper around a short question and contribution statement, a single current protocol, the GB10 results, and limitations; move review history, superseded numbers, P2 rows, and audit implementation details to an appendix or supplement.

There is a concrete reproducibility inconsistency in the released package. The manuscript repeatedly states “44 cited / 44 found / 0 missing,” whereas the shipped `results/s48_provenance.json` and `paper/PROVENANCE.md` contain 49 cited and 49 found entries, and `PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json` reports “50/50/0.” The underlying records are present, but these contradictory counts mean the manuscript is not synchronized with the artifact it asks readers to trust. Regenerate the manuscript, provenance table, manifest, and all audit summaries together and remove stale fields (the review-verification artifact also retains an older `manuscript_claim` value).

The definition of the main diagnostic needs a correctness-level repair. The paper defines

$$
\rho=\max_i\frac{|R_{\mathrm{def},i}-R_{64,i}|}{10^{-4}+10^{-4}|R_{64,i}|}
$$

but states that $\rho\geq1$ and `frac_over = 0` are exact statements about the benchmark's own allclose check. The evaluator-scaled tolerance used by the benchmark scales the relative term with the candidate, not with the fp64 anchor in this diagnostic. The paper reports zero verdict disagreements on the measured rows and a small observed delta, which supports an empirical equivalence on those rows; it does not make the two predicates identical in general. Call $(\rho,\mathrm{frac\_over})$ an anchor-normalized diagnostic throughout, and reserve “passes the benchmark contract” for the evaluator-exact candidate-versus-reference check. The scalar counterexample in §4.1 currently mixes the reference diagnostic with candidate admission and reinforces this ambiguity.

The candidate-admission headline is much weaker than its placement suggests. The declared-feature model reaches 96.4286% accuracy exactly because it matches the two-line `torch/torch.compile` versus hand-arm rule; it adds no accuracy over that trivial baseline. Within the hand-fp32 stratum, the measured-feature-free AUC is 0.7548 with a clustered 95% interval whose lower endpoint is 0.537, and zero of 20 positives crosses the stated 0.5 cut. Thus admission is not an execution-free certificate: class information is partly measured, and the operating threshold remains per-shape measurement. The abstract and introduction should present this as a limitation and baseline characterization, not as a general predictive result. The single recorded input per shape and one-process dispatch repeats also limit any claim about correctness or dispatch stability outside this validity domain; the scope section should make those limits prominent when discussing deployment.

The paper can become publishable after a substantial rewrite that (i) cites and quantitatively distinguishes the three 2026 KernelBench-checker papers, (ii) presents only the current GB10 contribution and removes the review chronology from the main text, (iii) synchronizes every artifact and provenance count, and (iv) makes the anchor-normalized versus evaluator-exact predicates mathematically consistent. The GB10 dispatch/error observations and the carefully bounded negative findings are worth preserving, but the submitted version does not yet meet the novelty, clarity, and reproducibility standards for acceptance.
