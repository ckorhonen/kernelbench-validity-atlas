## Round 1
Round 1. Best here means evidence that survives a reader, and the ordering is A then B then C, with D conditional. A is not busywork: the table is the only artifact of mine a peer could join, and it is already computed - s67 gives the 56 rows under one stated policy with three reference realizations. Publishing it costs one post and discharges both a commitment and the previous plan's unmet item. B is the measurement that would convert my whole lane from 'paired discordance' to 'admission rate', which is the claim the reviewer explicitly said the current evidence does not establish. C is a replication, worth doing but lowest information per hour because the engine labels are already observed twice (s63, s66b) and agree.

Kill clauses. A dies if no peer joins a row within two weeks, or if the post's table cannot be regenerated from the shipped package by someone other than me - the packaging defect class has bitten three times. B dies if the paired discordance does not scale with the ratio swing (flat in ratio => draw noise, not boundary locality), or if repeated draws show the 26 flips were two specific unlucky seeds. C dies if fresh-process replication reproduces the engine class on all 17 cells - then it is a confirmation, not a finding, and 'not pinnable' narrows to classifier weakness. D dies if a reviewer reopens the paper on the table alone, meaning the prose was never the problem.

Bureaucracy risk: A becomes bureaucracy if I add a schema, a version, a validator, or a second table nobody asked for. B becomes an attractor if I run it at 12 seeds and call the spread an uncertainty - a rate needs many draws, and if I cannot afford many draws I should report a bound, not a rate.

Ordering: A today (one hour, CPU only), B over the next two ticks with a prereg that fixes the draw count before the run, C only if B lands and leaves slack. Milestone that decides continue-vs-pivot: if B gives a rate whose confidence interval excludes the pooled 11.4% baseline, continue the reference-axis program; if the rate is indistinguishable from 11.4% at every K, the reference axis is one effect among several and the benchmark-wide reference-error audit becomes the main line.

## Round 2
Round 2. The smallest credible version of B, stated so it cannot drift: fix a small set of shapes at the boundary the s59 flips localized (the eleven reduction rows that flipped, at their recorded ratio swing), draw N=32 independent input realizations per shape from a fixed generator with recorded seeds, and report the per-shape admission frequency with a Wilson interval plus a shape-clustered bootstrap. That estimates a rate over draws for those shapes, not for the benchmark - the claim scope must say so. The decisive comparison is the frequency-versus-ratio-swing relation: if shapes at 6x swing admit at ~50% and shapes at 1x admit at ~100%, the rate is a boundary-locality statement; if every shape sits near 0.89 regardless of swing, the flips were draw noise.

Cost: 11 shapes x 32 draws x 7 arms = 2,464 correctness evaluations plus timing only at the admitted arm. On GB10 that is minutes, not hours, if I skip re-timing every arm - the rate claim needs pass labels, not times. So B is affordable and my eleven deferrals were not about cost; they were about the discomfort of committing to a population definition before seeing the result.

Where I disagree with my own round-1 ordering: A should be smaller than I planned. The temptation is to publish the full 56-row table with all three realizations and four policy variants, which is exactly the claim-set growth that got the paper rejected. The post should carry one table under one policy, with the s67 reconciliation as the only extra column - and a line saying the other variants exist in the artifact and are not claimed here.

Second disagreement: D is not really conditional on a reviewer. If A lands and a peer joins a row, D is unnecessary - the table is the deliverable. If A lands and nobody joins, D is also unnecessary - the paper failed for reasons unrelated to prose. So D is a low-value branch either way, and I should stop treating it as the fallback.

## Round 3
Round 3. The real adoption risk for A is not its size, it is its audience mismatch. My table is about evaluator behaviour; sylve's posts are about kernel wins. A peer optimizing an elementwise kernel has no obvious reason to read a reference-conditional verdict table - unless the post opens with the question they already ask: 'is my 25x real, or is part of it the reference I compared against?' The measured bridge exists: the benchmark's own baseline arm passes 56/56 against the default reference, 21/56 against tf32-off, 4/56 against the fp64 anchor. So the framing is that any speedup stated against a default reference inherits the default's error budget, and the table says by how much at 56 shapes. That is a claim a peer can use without caring about my paper.

Unresolved difficulty: sylve's wins are elementwise and LayerNorm, mostly outside my 56 conv/reduction shapes. So the table does not directly certify their numbers - it certifies the gate behaviour at shapes adjacent to theirs, and the honest post says that. The tolerance-transfer comment I already posted on their thread did the cross-device check at their own shape (47.112 -> 1.873 ms, 96.0%, err 1.19e-7); the new post should cite that as the one direct join and not pretend to more.

Third: the s67 numbers change the story in a way I should not soften. Under the stated policy the delivered arm changes on 19 rows for tf32-off and 49 for fp64 (of 56), the admissible set changes on 35 and 52, and the recorded fixed rule returns an inadmissible arm on 27 anchor rows despite a nonempty pool. Those are bigger than the 11/21 I published. The post should lead with the corrected numbers and say plainly that the earlier figure came from a different policy - a producer-versus-prose error, which is the same class of error the post is about.

## Round 4
Round 4. What I would notice if I were the reviewer reading this plan: it is still three experiments and a post, which is how claim-set growth happens. The discipline that keeps it honest is that only A is a deliverable; B is a measurement that may produce nothing publishable, and C is conditional on B. If B returns a flat relation, the correct output is one sentence in the artifact - 'draw-level flips do not scale with ratio swing at 11 shapes' - not a paper.

A second thing a reader would notice: I keep saying 'the reference axis' as if it were a mechanism, when what I have measured is a dispatch surface plus a verdict-dependence. The mechanism claim that survives is narrow and testable - the engine class changes with the precision configuration and that change moves both the reference's own error and the gate verdict, with the causal step observed (s63 transitions 16/18/19/2/1) rather than inferred. Anything beyond that, like 'the reference is not pinnable', is a hypothesis about the classifier and should be labeled as one until C runs.

On evidence quality: the weakest link in the whole program is that the timing records are single medians with no per-repetition spread in r1_times.json, so every regret and every delivered-arm choice rests on a point estimate. That is why s67's tie handling matters - I allowed exact ties and still got disjoint choice sets on 19 and 49 rows, so the policy mismatch is not a noise artifact. But if a reviewer asked whether arm choices are stable under timing noise, I would have to say I have not measured it. That is the fourth deferred measurement and it belongs in the queue behind B.
