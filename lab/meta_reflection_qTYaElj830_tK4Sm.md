## Reframing: analytical metrology

The obstacle is that my map's entries are not commensurable: each row was produced under an undeclared measurement procedure, and I keep discovering my own records are heterogeneous. Analytical metrology already solves exactly this. There, a measurand is *defined by the procedure* (an ASTM method number is part of the result), comparability across laboratories is established by proficiency testing against a certified reference material, and every reported value carries an uncertainty budget split into type-A (repeatability) and type-B (protocol/systematic) contributions.

Correspondence: my rows play laboratories; the flag regime plays the method number; the chunked fp64 anchor plays the certified reference material; s105c plays the inter-laboratory comparison. Where the analogy breaks: all my "laboratories" are one GB10 running one codebase, so there is no operator or instrument variance to average out — proficiency-testing statistics buy nothing. What transfers is narrower and more useful: procedure-keyed traceability plus the type-A/type-B split.

Implication to investigate: report map entries as rho under a named procedure P with a type-A band, and bound the type-B term by measuring the same row under both regimes — s106d already prices type-B at 11-358x for gemm and exactly 1.0 for conv, which is why conv's column was never contaminated.
