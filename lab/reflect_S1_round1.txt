S1-ROUND1 (974 words):

1. The central claim, restated as a conditional. "Given a recorded reference realization and a tolerance
budget, a candidate kernel's benchmark verdict is a binary whole-tensor check against that realization (the
contract), and any statement about the reference's own consumption of that budget (rho_max, frac_over) is a
diagnostic on the reference, not an admission predicate: the candidate's verdict is decided by the benchmark's
own check, never by an anchor statistic." The paper claims this certificate form is what the benchmark
community lacks: published tables report a speedup against one reference realization and treat a pass as if
the reference were exact. Our data (56-row conv union + 672 arm-anchor cells + 6-day drift) show the
reference realization is part of the claim: the baseline torch arm fails its own check on 21/56 rows under
ieee and 52/56 against the fp64 anchor, while the same candidate-versus-reference check on the hand arm
passes on 0/4 conv rows, and the diagnostic pair (frac_over 0.0052 winograd vs 0.778 TF32) correctly
separates rows whose ratios are interpretable from rows whose ratios are meaningless.

2. Strongest evidence. Three legs, all shipped and auditable from inside the package.
(a) Reference-realization axis (A6/A7/A8): rho is a measured surface over (family, depth, geometry, engine
class), not a depth law — matmul crosses rho=1 at K 3608-3632, but the 66-row, 6-seed neighborhood study
(A10) shows the crossing is a coin flip (4/6 and 2/6 pass, no monotone seed), and conv splits into a
winograd class (rho 10.195, frac_over 0.0052, bit-identical under every flag) and a TF32 class (rho 251.69,
frac_over 0.778, flag removes 99.9%) — a categorical collapse, not a continuous surface. These are the two
legs of the spine's negative: the continuous collapse fails, the categorical form succeeds.
(b) The instrument before the science: calibration (48 SMs, 24MB L2, launch floor 7.46us), interleaved
A/B with L2 flush, day-2 drift <=0.13% — so every boundary claim carries its validity domain (waves,
bytes/thread, wave-pinned CTA counts, launch-bound K).
(c) The selector as studied object (E2b/W4/S56/D1): the zero-probe gate is oracle-tied on 75% of
out-of-sample cells (0/144 worse-than-1% transfer), probes cost more than the rule's entire residual,
autotune amortizes only after ~140-260 visits/shape; the honest delivery frontier is gate-plus-compile
where compile's one-time cost V-structure is bounded.
On all of this the paper's central claim is not a ranking of kernels but a conditional: the certificate
certifies benchmark agreement at the recorded realization plus the diagnostic pair; the same data that
support the certificate (the three criteria, the anchor lattice, the admission rule keyed on frac_over)
also show that delivery claims without an admission field are rankings.

3. The two measurements that would settle or substantially revise it.
(A) A second architecture with a real precision knob (e.g., an sm_90+ with a documented TF32/fp16 path) —
the two-class dispatch result is currently a statement about cudnn's menu on sm_120; the anchor lattice and
the frac_over diagnostic generalize only where the reference's engine menu has the same two classes. Kill:
a second arch shows baseline admissibility trivially satisfied => the two-class structure is a GB10 artifact.
(B) A controlled input-distribution sweep at fixed shape (the L1/40 LayerNorm row, A11, is the first of
these): the reference's budget consumption rises near-linearly in row conditioning (rho 0.011 at kappa 1.73
to 2.529 at kappa 1000), so the diagnostic pair's magnitude field is a function of input conditioning,
not of the candidate. Kill: a family whose rho is below 0.1 yet whose verdicts flip under anchor swap =>
rho is not the mechanism.

4. The resubmission findings, in one sentence each, and what they collectively say the paper was missing.
F1 (tuple-test pad column): the paper's primary measurement apparatus (tuple_test.py) did not execute the
intervention it described; the review's arithmetic reproduction caught a no-op. The program was missing
artifact-level specificity: the code that ran, with the exact padding it applied, in the package.
F2 (discrete boundary vs simple surface): the prose overclaimed a projection result as a boundary law; the
0.604 nearest-centroid value is a statement about one coordinate family, not about all simple predictors.
The paper was missing honest scope: the line between 'the measurement supports X' and 'the measurement is
the only evidence for X'.
F3 (certificate conflation): the paper's §2.4/§4.4 inferred candidate failure from the reference's bulk
failure; the reviewer's counterexample (reference fails bulk, candidate passes the benchmark contract) is
the cleanest possible demonstration. The paper was missing a separation of criteria — the three-way split
in §4.1.
F4 (reproduction exceeds supplied implementation): the paper described experiments (tuple test, S56 rerun,
producers) that the package did not contain; the reviewer reproduced arithmetic from committed records but
could not reproduce the measurement. The paper was missing a runnable packaging layer: scripts that run
from inside the package, gzip-aware row-streams, seed-derived inputs, determinism checks that re-run the
delivered hand computation.
F5 (selector economics not correctness-conditioned): the paper's own §6.3 asserted a total-time ranking
that the corrected accounting (passing-only vs all-row means) refutes; the reviewer reproduced the
arithmetic. The paper was missing correctness-conditioned cost accounting. Together these five findings
say, plainly: the paper had the numbers right (reviewers reproduced every mean and t-statistic) and the
claims wrong (the interpretive layer overreached the numbers and the package).
