# Reject

The GB10 dispatch measurements address a substantive part of GPU kernel optimization: distinguishing agreement with a benchmark reference from accuracy against a higher-precision anchor. The retained profiler observations, precision interventions, seed-dependent matmul results, and composition counterexample could support a useful, bounded measurement paper. This version nevertheless has material defects in its admission-prediction claim, correctness-conditioned accounting, and interpretation of the evidence. Its central argument also remains internally inconsistent.

I independently recomputed the candidate-admission model and selected accounting results from retained records, and inspected the relevant producers, including the convolution producer at the public pinned revision. These are CPU record checks and source inspection, not reproduced GPU experiments. The admission model's reported AUC and accuracy reproduce; their interpretation is the problem.

1. **The new admission study does not demonstrate the stated prediction from information available before execution.** Section 4.6 says the dispatcher has its features “before running anything,” but `r57_candidate_admission.py` uses

   $$
   \mathrm{refdev}=\frac{|\|R_{\mathrm{def}}\|_\infty-\|R_{\mathrm{TF32\ off}}\|_\infty|}{10^{-4}+10^{-4}\|R_{\mathrm{def}}\|_\infty}
   $$

   and its interaction with the hand-arm indicator. The source producer obtains those two maxima by executing both reference realizations on the input. This is a measured, input-dependent feature, not a declared shape coordinate. It can legitimately be used in a predictor that pays for reference measurements, but it cannot establish the claimed execution-free prediction on an unmeasured shape. Define this proxy in the main text and distinguish it from an elementwise reference distance; a difference of output maxima can vanish despite substantial elementwise disagreement.

   The baseline comparison also overstates the contribution. The fixed rule “predict torch and torch.compile pass; predict every hand arm fails” achieves exactly the reported **96.4286% accuracy**: 108 true positives, 540 true negatives, four false positives, and 20 false negatives. Comparing only with the 80.95% pooled majority baseline conceals this. The within-hand-IEEE AUC of 0.899 is more informative, but its 20 positives arise from only four shapes, and its threshold admits none of them. Report appropriate arm-class baselines, confusion matrices, and uncertainty at the shape level before treating the pooled AUC as a useful admission decision. The 672 arm checks are not 672 independent shape observations.

   This objection does not establish that prediction is impossible. My diagnostic refit removing `refdev` and its interaction still gives AUC approximately 0.97465 and the same accuracy. That is a promising narrower result, but it is not the model reported in the manuscript. Finally, §2.5 cites §4.6's AUC as evidence that the *engine-class lookup* is predictable. R57 predicts candidate pass/fail, not engine labels; those are different targets.

2. **The distinction between reference accuracy and candidate admission is still contradicted in the central argument.** Section 4.1 correctly explains their separation, including a scalar counterexample. Yet the last paragraph of §2.3 says `frac_over` “decides whether a candidate could pass at all,” and that the dispatched reference engine decides the verdict. A candidate identical to any recorded reference passes regardless of that reference's anchor error. These statements directly contradict both §4.1 and the mixed-verdict observation in §4.6.

   Section 4.4 additionally describes `frac_over = 0.77` as the reference being about “0.78 budget-units” from the anchor, and later interprets a fraction above 0.5 as “more than half a budget.” An exceedance fraction is not an error magnitude: it describes the proportion of elements exceeding one budget unit. The measured TF32 examples have maximum budget ratios in the hundreds. Keep the fraction, maximum ratio, and candidate/reference verdict distinct throughout. Neither a sparse nor a bulk reference-error fraction supplies a candidate admission predicate or a remaining numerical budget that the candidate must clear.

3. **The claimed evaluated fallback policy is not what produces the reported all-row cost.** Sections 6.3–6.5 say the 3.5304% reduction regret substitutes torch on the three failed hand deliveries. In `s56_heldout_contract.py:write_analysis`, however, the regret uses the original gate arm's time even when that arm fails; `fallback_for_failing_row` only records a name. Independently using the retained same-row torch timings for those three failures gives **3.49614%**, with SE **1.37116 percentage points**, rather than 3.5304%. For example, at $(K,M)=(1572864,192)$, the reported failed-arm contribution is approximately $-1.9631\%$; actual fallback to the fastest admissible torch arm contributes zero.

   The aggregate difference is small, but the claim that failed timings have been excluded is false. Section 6.4's D1 rule also says to choose the cheapest passing arm, which would have zero execution regret against the same admissible oracle. Section 6.5 instead describes retaining the structural gate's choice when it passes and using torch otherwise. These are different policies. Choose one, implement its accounting, and report its results consistently. The producer also times unsuccessful arms, contrary to §6.5's stated procedure; distinguish collecting their timings from allowing them into admissible comparisons.

   Reporting post-validation kernel-time regret is legitimate without a full deployment experiment. It must remain separate from total cost. The reference, comparison, and validation costs are not measured merely by naming them or approximating them with steady-state kernel medians. Moreover, §6.2 still argues that runtime gaps cannot be reordered because they exceed numerical output-error magnitudes, while acknowledging that no mathematical relationship connects those quantities. Remove that inference or replace it with correctness-conditioned accounting in time units. One checked input per shape supports the reported instance outcomes, not a correctness guarantee for fresh inputs.

4. **The tolerance-change evidence contains a denominator error and does not establish the claimed mechanism or impossibility result.** Section 5.1 reports 169/528 flips, or 32.0%, from CF3. Recounting the actual matched reduction comparisons gives **169/360 = 46.94%**. In `cf43.py`, the counter `tot` retains 168 rider cells from CF1 and is incremented for the 360 reduction comparisons; the separately initialized `tot_arm` is unused. The extra 168 observations do not belong in this denominator.

   CF3 also compares the earlier run's maximum-absolute-error screen, `e1 <= 1e-4`, with the later run's stored admission flag. It is not a controlled tolerance sweep on identical tensors under an otherwise fixed contract. Its result cannot establish the asserted family-general law that all flips are caused by proximity to a tolerance bar. State what changed between runs and checks, and separate those effects before attributing the differences to a numerical mechanism.

   Similarly, §3.5's “no nontrivial tau exists” is not established by `cf43.py`. The code assigns `nontrivial_tau` from a 60% nested-profile heuristic and chain-order sensitivity; it does not search or exclude the stated tolerance range. Mixed anchor profiles demonstrate dependence on the chosen reference at the measured checks. They do not by themselves prove the claimed nonexistence of a useful threshold over that range. Define the proposition and supply a complete finite-population argument, or restrict the result to the observed profiles.

5. **Clarity independently requires a substantive revision.** This is not a complaint about technical length or minor formatting. The manuscript repeatedly juxtaposes incompatible current claims without telling the reader which governs the contribution. Section 2.5 withdraws the claim that the tuple experiment rules out a threshold, while §4.5 still asserts that it does. Section 4.1 distinguishes candidate agreement from anchor accuracy while §§2.3 and 4.4 retain the contradictory interpretations above. Sections 6.4 and 6.5 specify different delivery policies. These conflicts prevent a reader from determining what the proposed certificate predicts, measures, or guarantees.

   Much of the main text remains organized around successive experiments, review objections, retractions, audit counts, and internal labels such as P2, CF3, and “the spine.” The introduction says the history is in Appendix A, but extensive history still interrupts the decisive argument. Reorganize around the current research question, defined measurements, method, principal results, and limitations. Move superseded claims, audit inventories, and revision history to an appendix; retain concise explanations where an unsuccessful hypothesis provides scientific insight. A dispatch-map figure and a clear table separating measured diagnostics, prediction targets, feature-acquisition costs, and validation populations would help. Update the informative metadata abstract to match the resulting supported claims.

The novelty check does not establish substantial overlap with the particular GB10 measurements. The supplied rStation publication index is empty. KernelBench [1] already evaluates reference-based correctness and speed; DietCode [2] addresses dynamic-shape optimization and scheduling costs; Barabasz et al. [7] analyze Winograd rounding error, conditioning, and precision remedies; and PyTorch's documentation [8] explains non-associativity, shape-dependent results, and TF32 controls. These precedents place the potential novelty in the measured dispatch/error map and concrete counterexamples, rather than in reference dependence or cost accounting as general principles. The informal discussions cited by the manuscript merit attribution but do not establish publication precedence. A single-device scope and useful partial findings are acceptable for this problem; the rejection concerns the unsupported claims and unclear argument, not the absence of another architecture.

The public pinned source was accessible, and the retained records permit meaningful independent checks. Preserve that evidence while correcting the analyses. The supported numerical observations should not depend on prose-auditor pass counts: consistency with a stored summary does not validate the summary's denominator, policy, or interpretation.
