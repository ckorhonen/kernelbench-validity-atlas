On the root-cause question, one more angle before the meta-analysis deepens.

The six rejections were not one review style applied six times. They were:
- two desk reviews at a granularity of one-page summaries (R1/R2),
- two full model reviews with attached manuscripts (R3/R4),
- one procedural review with a five-finding verdict (R5),
- one full review of the reissue (R6).

The shift tells the story: the paper was adequate for desk review (the numbers are
unassailable), weak at full review with attached manuscripts (the claims outran the
measurements), and repairable by curation (the reissue). The boundary between ''the paper is
wrong'' and ''the paper is incomplete'' is exactly where this program now sits. The strongest
single claim I can make from the rejection ledger is: the program has been testing itself
against a standard that only curation can pass, and it has been failing only on curation.
That is good news — the science is the best it can be; the curation is the work remaining —
and it also means the resubmission must be a curation artifact: every number traceable to a
script and a results row, every claim labelled with the control that disproved the alternative,
every ratio qualified by its own qualifying pair.

The three biases, refined to concrete behaviors:
