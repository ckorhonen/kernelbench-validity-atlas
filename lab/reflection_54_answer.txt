NEW REFLECTION — an opposing school.

The incumbent program in this problem, mine included, holds that what prevents progress is measurement: the field reports point verdicts at a single reference realization and a single tolerance reading, so claims are conditional on unrecorded choices. My answer has been to map the condition — a validity-domain atlas with reference realization as an axis, rho and frac_over as derived coordinates, and a certificate that names the completion it assumes.

The opposing school I want to develop says the obstacle is not measurement but specification, and that the right object is not a surface but a survival measure. Call it the completion school. Its claim: a kernel claim is a partial function of deployment parameters the artifact never records — reference build, admission convention, probe budget, repeat-visit count, tolerance direction — and the apparent disagreements in this field are completions of one claim, not empirical disputes. What prevents progress is that each lab measures one completion and reports it as the claim. What would make progress possible is to treat the completion set as the object of study and report, per claim, the measure of completions on which its verdict changes.

The prerequisite it questions is the one my program rests on: that you must measure a kernel to know its behavior, and that a boundary measured on a shape lattice is a fact about the kernel. The completion school says the lattice is one coordinate of a larger object, and that a number measured at an unrecorded completion has no stable meaning — it is a reading of the recording convention as much as of the kernel. It also questions my preferred representation: an atlas predicts over shapes; a survival table does not predict at all, it audits. And it questions the intermediate goal both sylve's work and mine treat as the deliverable — the speedup number, or the boundary. Under this school those are outputs of a completion; the deliverable is the fragility.

Mechanism, not name: enumerate the completions that are recorded-absent but physically reachable, re-evaluate the same claim at each, and report the fraction of the completion set where the verdict's sign changes. The derived quantity is completion fragility. It is not a variance estimate — variance is over repetitions at a fixed completion; fragility is over completions at a fixed measurement.

Worked case, from my own artifacts. My paper's conv delivery claim, measured at completion {reference=default, admission=published}, delivers torch on 52 of 56 rows. Change one completion — reference realized at fp64 — and 21 rows change arm while 31 lose admissible delivery entirely (results/r45_rider_adm.json, verified from per_row rather than the summary field). Change the admission convention to admissible-on-every-arm and 24 of 56 lose delivery at the ieee rung. The claim's headline survives; its per-row content does not. The sharpest case is my own V2 certificate-health check: under a completion that omitted the |ref| column it returned pass-frac 0.0 on both families (results/s6_dispatcher_audit.json), a verdict that read as a mechanism result and was in fact a missing column. Obeying the frozen rule was correct procedure, and the number was still about the recording, not the kernel.

First serious difficulty: the completion set is combinatorial and mostly unmeasured, and measuring it costs GPU time proportional to its size — exactly the probe economics I already measured, with autotune break-even at 15-260 repeat visits per shape. So the school does not remove cost, it relocates it from model-building to re-measurement. The refinement that keeps it tractable is to enumerate only completions that are physically reachable and recorded-absent, and declare the rest. The organizing assumption to reconsider if that fails: that fragility is a property of the claim. If fragility is mostly convention-driven — the same rows flipping under every completion — then the deliverable is a reporting standard, not a measurement program, and the school's ambition shrinks to a schema.

Scope. Established as a worked case: the completions I have actually measured on GB10 with my own artifacts — reference lattice, admission convention, probe budget, tolerance direction (the last turned out inert: 0/350 and 0/816 label flips). Conjectural: that fragility generalizes to other problems and other architectures. Open: whether fragility is predictable.

Comparison with the strongest available work. Sylve's reference-order results are single-completion measurements reported as bit-identity claims; her stated limitation ("no separate oracle") is an unrecorded completion, and the completion school reads that limitation as the interesting object rather than as the caveat. Ruachtov's CUPTI counters are the strongest counter-argument: if hardware counters certify same-work, the completion set collapses and fragility is zero by construction. But counters cannot see which arithmetic tree produced the work, so his invariance sits at the wrong level to settle a tolerance claim — he can certify the same FLOPs ran, not that the same arithmetic did. My incumbent program is, on this reading, a partial completion school: it enumerates exactly one axis, reference realization, then tries to predict a surface over shapes. The question that separates us is whether fragility is predictable from my derived coordinates. CF3 says it partly is: 169/528 pass flips, all at budget ratios 1.1-1.8e-4, while a pair at 1.2e-7 never flips. If margin-to-bar predicts fragility, my atlas is right and fragility is one more coordinate; if it does not, the completion school is right and the deliverable is a survival table.

Decision and the investigation I run now. The completion that produced V2's 0.0 pass-frac is precisely the missing |ref| column on the held-out rows — also the reason the per-arm table has been deferred three times. So the discriminating experiment is already owed: re-run the 34 held-out shapes' arms on spark, record reference magnitudes and evaluator-exact errors per row, and recompute certificate health under the rtol-scaled contract together with the per-arm admission table. Two outcomes frozen before the run: pass-frac >= 0.9 means V2's verdict was a completion artifact and the completion school's worked case replicates on a second artifact; pass-frac < 0.5 means the gate genuinely delivers failing arms and my conditional-certificate claim stands as written. I will record the school in SCHOOL_COMPLETION.md and touch §5.3 only if the first outcome holds — the paper is under review, so an edit needs a reason.

Reconsider-if: if fragility turns out predictable from margin-to-bar across every completion I can measure, the completion school collapses into my atlas as one coordinate and I stop treating it as an alternative. If it stays unpredictable, the atlas is a lookup table wearing a theory's clothes, and §5 should say so.
