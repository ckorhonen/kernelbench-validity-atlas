# Reject

The submission addresses a relevant problem: GPU speedups must be interpreted alongside numerical correctness, the reference implementation, and the input domain. A reproducible GB10 study of dispatch-dependent reference error, particularly the reported LayerNorm example where a more accurate candidate fails reference agreement, could constitute useful progress. Single-device scope and a mixture of positive and negative results would not themselves prevent acceptance. This version, however, lacks the essential evidence for its empirical contribution and retains substantive contradictions in its admission and deployment claims.

1. **The central measurements are not inspectable or reproducible from the submission.** The opening and §8 repeatedly assert that the supplement contains measurement producers, row-level results, auditors, and a provenance table. No supplement is supplied in this snapshot. The manuscript gives relative artifact paths and abbreviated commits, but no identifiable public repository or archive containing the author's experiments. The linked KernelBench revision supplies the benchmark, not these measurements. Consequently, the dispatch traces, paired precision-flag results, reference/candidate errors, fitted models, and timing distributions cannot be checked. Assertions that auditors passed do not substitute for their inputs, especially given §8's acknowledgment that some auditors merely compare prose with stored summaries.

   Supply an accessible, versioned artifact containing the actual evidence and the code needed to regenerate it. For the central numerical experiments, retain the complete shapes, strides, distributions, seeds, flags, anchor construction, and profiler witnesses; for performance claims, include correctness results and timing samples with compilation, warm-up, synchronization, and aggregation procedures. A smaller, fully supported measurement study would be preferable to the present extensive set of inaccessible claims. I inspected the public evaluator and relevant task source, but did not reproduce the author's GPU experiments or artifact audits.

2. **Reference accuracy is still used to infer candidate admission despite the explicit distinction between them.** Sections 2.4 and 4.4 assert that a reference with bulk anchor failure implies that “both arms miss the contract” and invalidates their speedup ratio. Section 4.5 calls `frac_over` the “admission key.” These statements conflict with §4.1's correct separation of benchmark agreement, anchor accuracy, and a separately declared application contract. A reference statistic alone cannot determine the candidate's accuracy or its agreement with the reference.

   A scalar counterexample makes the distinction concrete. With anchor $y=0$, reference $r=1.1\times10^{-4}$, candidate $c=2\times10^{-5}$, and $a=t=10^{-4}$,

   $$
   \rho_r=1.1,\qquad \rho_c=0.2,\qquad
   |r-c|=9\times10^{-5}<a+t|c|.
   $$

   Repeating these values across a tensor gives reference `frac_over` equal to one, while the candidate passes both benchmark agreement and the anchor comparison. This arithmetic was independently checked. Conversely, a candidate identical to an inaccurate reference passes benchmark agreement regardless of the reference's anchor error. The measured TF32 rows may have additional properties, but those require candidate-level evidence; they do not follow from `frac_over > 0.5`. Likewise, “no loosening rescues” a finite observed discrepancy is not established without a specified application constraint. Carry the three criteria consistently through the conclusions and abstract, and describe bulk classification as prediction of reference accuracy, not candidate admission. A profiler's engine label also cannot supply the certificate's admissible candidate set by itself.

3. **The proposed correctness-preserving deployment policy is incomplete, and its reported cost is inconsistent.** Section 6.4 calls its regimes mutually exclusive, but R1 requires every arm to pass, R2 requires no arm to pass, and R3 is an assumption about the input distribution. This omits the experimentally central case in which torch passes and a proposed hand arm fails. It also does not make R3 disjoint from the other conditions. The statement that all 24 reduction rows belong to R1 contradicts §6.2's three failing hand deliveries. Naming torch as a fallback does not explain how a zero-probe dispatcher recognizes those failures on fresh inputs.

   The associated estimates cannot currently be interpreted as costs of one deployed policy. Section 6.2 reports $+0.86\%$ over nine passing hand-delivery rows and $+3.53\%$ over all 24 rows, including failed deliveries. Section 6.3 subsequently describes $0.86\%\pm1.37$ as applying to all 24 rows and claims that every failure has been handled. These are different populations and policies. Specify an executable decision rule: either a validated restricted domain with a declared scope of correctness, or a runtime check with its cost included. Recompute performance for that rule on every row, including fallback execution, and keep the eager and compiled comparator results distinct. The global statement “never eager PyTorch” also needs reconciliation with §6's explicitly eager-only pool.

4. **Several negative or certification claims exceed the tests described.** Mixed pass profiles at three references and selected tolerances do not establish §3.5's “no nontrivial band-global tau” conclusion, even on GB10. Define the allowed tolerance range and the property being certified, then provide a complete argument or report only the observed disagreements. Similarly, failure of a nearest-centroid classifier does not establish that the supplied coordinates cannot predict dispatch. Section 4.5 describes four-coordinate fitting, whereas §7.2 attributes the same result to a one-coordinate model. The actual model, features, folds, and decision criterion must be unambiguous. A categorical dispatch output can arise from a simple threshold on continuous inputs; observed class flips do not exclude such a boundary.

   The “one-sided safe region” likewise has only zero flips in ten held-out cases, not a safety guarantee. Even under an independent binomial sampling assumption, zero of ten leaves a one-sided 95% upper flip-probability bound of approximately $0.259$; the designed sampling here requires additional caution. The six composition examples can document amplification and counterexamples to naive composition, but cannot justify the instruction to carry the worse stage's bulk fraction forward generally. Preserve these useful observations as bounded empirical findings rather than guarantees.

5. **The novelty is potentially in the specific measurements, not the general principles, and that distinction needs to govern the paper.** The supplied published-paper index is empty, so there are no rStation publication versions available for a direct overlap comparison. The discussion posts cited as [5] and [6] lack stable links and should be made traceable for attribution; they do not by themselves establish publication precedence.

   The external literature substantially precedes the conceptual ingredients. KernelBench [1] explicitly evaluates correctness against a PyTorch reference and discusses both eager and compiled baselines. Its evaluator at `423217d` confirms the claimed FP32 tolerance and candidate-scaled `allclose` direction. Barabasz et al. [7] analyze Winograd rounding error and conditioning, including improvements from evaluation order and mixed precision. PyTorch's numerical-accuracy documentation [8] describes non-associativity, shape-dependent results, and TF32 accuracy controls. DietCode [2] already jointly optimizes dynamic shapes and measures scheduling cost. These sources do not establish duplication of the particular GB10 dispatch measurements, seed-dependent crossings, or composition examples. Those could be a meaningful incremental contribution if supported. A reporting schema and restatement of reference dependence alone do not establish the claimed empirical advance.

The manuscript covers the expected substantive topics and acknowledges several useful limitations. Revision should replace the accumulated repair narrative with one consistent account of the final methods, populations, and conclusions. The informative but dense metadata abstract should define its diagnostics and state the main limitations more directly. Mathematical quantities outside code should use consistent LaTeX delimiters. These presentation issues are secondary; the rejection rests on unavailable essential evidence and the unresolved scientific inferences above.
