## 4.1 The certificate, stated so that a third party can evaluate it

A results row in this benchmark is a candidate kernel, a recorded reference, and a tolerance budget, scored by
an elementwise allclose. The published practice reports a speedup against that reference and treats a pass as
two-place. We propose the row carry a certificate with four fields, each of which is a measurement, not a
judgment:

1. **Reference realization** — which of {default, explicit-ieee, fp64-anchor} the row is scored against, plus
   the runtime flags in force (`cudnn.allow_tf32`, `matmul.allow_tf32`, `float32_precision`).
2. **Budget consumption** — the pair (rho_max, frac_over) at the row's shape (§2.3). These are
   **diagnostic**, not a substitute for the benchmark's own verdict, and the distinction is the load-bearing
   correction in this section. The benchmark's contract is a whole-tensor allclose against the recorded
   reference; that verdict is binary and is criterion (i). rho and frac_over measure a *different* comparison
   — the reference's distance from an fp64 anchor — and are criterion (ii). An explicitly relaxed application
   contract, if a paper wants one, is criterion (iii) and must be declared as an application requirement with
   its own threshold, not inferred from (ii). The three are reported separately because neither implies
   another: a reference 0.75e-4 from an exact anchor can pass (i) against a candidate 1.5e-4 that fails
   against the anchor, so frac_over = 0 does not license "no caveat needed." A scalar counterexample makes the separation concrete: anchor 0, reference 1.1e-4, candidate 2e-5, a = t = 1e-4 gives rho_r = 1.1 and rho_c = 0.2 while |r - c| = 9e-5 < a + t|c|, so frac_over = 1 and the candidate still passes both (i) and (ii). Repeating those values across a tensor gives a reference whose bulk fraction is one and a candidate that is admissible, which is why a reference statistic alone cannot decide admission. The earlier draft collapsed (i)
   and (ii) into one admission predicate, which is the defect this revision repairs.
3. **Admissible delivery set** — the arms that pass the contract at *this* realization, which for most rows is
   smaller than the set the rule selects.
4. **Regret basis** — the regret number and whether it is a lower bound, because a chosen arm that fails the
   contract cannot contribute a real gain.

The point of the form is that field 3 is the one the published tables omit, and it is the one that changes
conclusions.

## 4.2 Admissibility on every arm, including the baseline: the numbers

Under an enforced admission rule, with the reference realization as the axis (`results/r45_rider_adm.json`):

- The **baseline torch arm passes its own check** on 56/56 rows at default precision, **21/56** under ieee,
  and **4/56** against the fp64 anchor. A rule that admits the baseline unconditionally while demanding the
  candidate prove itself is therefore not symmetric, and on two of three realizations it is admitting an arm
  that fails.
- **Regret magnitudes survive the admission convention only at the default realization.** Paired changes
  recomputed in percentage points with max|·| (`results/s55_review_repair.json`; the published fields held
  fractional deltas under `paired_delta_pp_*` and a signed max, so "≤ 0.025 pp" was a unit error): def

  *ranking* of arms is unchanged at every realization — that part of the claim stands — but the magnitude
  bound does not: at ieee and fp64 a single row can move regret by tens of percentage points.
- **Delivery claims do not survive it.** Arm disagreement A2-vs-A1 is 0/24/31 (def/ieee/64); there is **no
  admissible delivery** on 24/56 rows (ieee) and 31/56 (64); and the admissible pool is empty — oracle
  undefined — on 20/56 (ieee) and 4/56 (64). These are the rows where the question "which arm wins" has no
  answer at that realization.
- The def→64 delivery-status change count is **52**, decomposing exactly into **21 arm→arm + 31 arm→none +
  0 none→arm**. The 21 matches our published count digit-for-digit; the 31 is the half we never reported, and
  it is delivery *loss* under enforced admission, not a change of winner. This is the F3 defect restated as
  arithmetic: our published "delivered arm changes" number was correct and incomplete.
- The certificate is **thin**: admissible-pool median size is 2/2/5 (def/ieee/64) with minimum 0. Said plainly
  because a certificate that is empty on a fifth of rows is a finding about the benchmark, not a defect in the
  certificate.

## 4.3 The one-sided screen, and the failed generalization that bounds it

A cushion screen asked whether a cheap pre-run statistic could predict which rows flip verdict under a
tolerance change (`lab/cushion_screen_prereg.md`, thresholds frozen pre-run; `results/cushion_screen.json`).
On the training set (n=360, 169 flips) the statistic reached AUC 0.743 with c* = 0.4578 and
P(flip | x ≤ c) = 0.0. On the rider set (n=560, 240 flips) it reached **AUC 0.121** — a failure, and the
pre-registered fail branch was taken without retry.

The consequence is a **one-sided safe region only**: when the statistic is below c*, the row does not flip
(0/10 rider rows below c* flipped); when it is above c*, nothing is claimed. The safe region is an observation, not a guarantee: zero flips in ten designed rows leaves a one-sided 95% upper bound of 0.259 on the flip probability under an independent-binomial reading (Clopper–Pearson), and the rider rows are designed rather than drawn from the deployment distribution, so the bound is looser still. There is no global ranking, and
no threshold that separates flippers from non-flippers on unseen rows. This is the third attempt in this
program to scalarize robustness into one number, and it failed the same way the other two did — by asking the
candidate to explain a property of the reference. §2.3's measurement asks the reference directly, which is why
it produced a two-class decomposition instead of a failed AUC.

## 4.4 What the certificate changes, and what it does not

**Changes.** Three quantities are kept apart here, because an earlier draft conflated them. `frac_over` is an
exceedance fraction — the share of the reference's output elements whose budget ratio is above 1 — not a
magnitude. The magnitude is the max budget ratio, which on TF32 conv rows sits in the hundreds (94.1-197.3 on
§6's conv rows, 429.4 on Table 1's channels-last cell). The candidate verdict is the benchmark's own
whole-tensor allclose against the recorded reference. At a row with frac_over 0.77 (any TF32 conv route) about
77% of the reference's elements are outside the budget and its worst element is hundreds of budgets away, so
the denominator of a speedup ratio is a reference that is itself far from an exact anchor — while the
remaining 23% of elements are inside, which is why such a row can still be evaluated at all. That is a
statement about the reference's accuracy, not a verdict on either arm: a candidate identical to that reference
passes. At a winograd row with frac_over 0.005 the ratio stands as measured, and the paper states that 0.5% of
reference elements are the ones the reference itself misses. On the reduction family, where the certificate is
clean at default precision (baseline 56/56), the published rankings stand unchanged — the certificate costs
nothing where it is not needed, which is the test of whether it is a ritual.

**Does not change.** The certificate does not tell you which kernel is faster. It tells you whether the
comparison was admissible, which is prior to the question. That is the deliberate demotion: §6 treats the
selector as a studied object precisely because a selector over inadmissible arms is a selector over noise.

"Targets and conventions." Three comparisons are kept separate and no threshold is implied by any single
statistic: (i) benchmark agreement — the row's own whole-tensor verdict against its recorded reference
(binary, at the recorded realization, and never inferred from frac_over); (ii) anchor accuracy — the
reference's distance from an fp64 anchor, reported as (rho_max, frac_over), diagnostic only; (iii) a
declared relaxed application contract, whose candidate-level threshold is stated by the applicant. The 0.02/0.5
frac_over lines are optional reporting conventions (tail-conditional / bulk-exceedance), not admission
criteria: frac_over = 0 means the reference is indistinguishable from an exact anchor at the budget, 0 <
frac_over < 0.02 means a tail of rare elements is outside the budget, and frac_over > 0.5 means the bulk of the
reference's output is outside it, so any ratio quoted against that reference has a denominator whose majority
is already outside the contract. The exceedance fraction is not a magnitude; the magnitude is the max budget
ratio, reported alongside it. What none of these decides is whether the candidate agrees with the reference,
which is (i), measured per row on the candidate. Bulk classification therefore predicts reference accuracy,
not candidate admission. Admissions claims carry the per-row candidate-versus-reference verdict
(results/r1_conv_gated.jsonl, results/w4_heldout.json.jsonl) together with the reference's (rho_max,
frac_over), and no speedup ratio is quoted against a row whose reference consumes bulk budget.

**P2 row.**
`{op_class: all_benchmark_rows, rule: every results row carries {reference_realization, rho_max, frac_over,
admissible_delivery_set, regret_basis}; a delivery claim requires the candidate to pass the benchmark's own
whole-tensor check against the recorded reference — the (rho_max, frac_over) pair is a diagnostic on the
reference and cannot substitute for that check, as in §4.1,
domain_predicate: certificate defined for every row; delivery claim defined iff candidate-vs-reference allclose
passes at 1e-4 (binary, whole-tensor); a relaxed contract, if declared, states its own candidate-level
threshold and justification rather than inheriting frac_over,
derived_coords: frac_over, rho_max, admissible_pool_size, boundary_ref: results/r45_rider_adm.json,
results/cushion_screen.json, results/a7_kernelclass.json, variance: regret bounds reported as paired
differences with n; AUC reported with its rider transfer, n_reps: 1 per row (deterministic records),
date: 2026-10-25}`

## 4.5 Is the certificate a surface or a lookup table? (A8, added tick #51)

The objection that decides whether §4 is a contribution or a ritual is whether (rho_max, frac_over) can be
predicted for an unmeasured shape. We ran that test as a preregistered compression test
(`lab/a8_compress_prereg.md` @ c56c0a0, frozen before the run; 48 rows over conv C{16,64,256} × four
dispatch classes × H{7,13,21} and matmul M{256,1024,4096} × K{128..8192}; artifacts
`results/a8_compress_v2.json`, `results/a8_analysis.json`, `results/a8b_analysis.json`).

The answer splits by field, and the split is the finding:

- **rho_max compresses in rank, not in magnitude.** Out-of-fold Spearman against measured log rho is 0.986
  with the engine class supplied as one categorical coordinate, and 0.833 from derived geometry alone
  (log k_eff, log n_elem, log C, log H) with no class at all. Rank is not calibration: the geometry-only
  model's median multiplicative prediction error is **6.84×** and its worst row is 156.7× off, while the
  corrected class+derived model — the one the 0.986 belongs to — gives median 1.251× and worst 2.409×, both
  refitted on `results/a8_compress_v2.json` and recomputed in `results/s55_review_repair.json`. A third pair
  (2.09×/440×) belongs to a superseded classification and is quoted only as that; a rank statistic from one
  model cannot be calibrated by another's residuals, so the numbers are reported per model rather than merged.
  The field is a surface for *ordering* and a lookup for *value*.
- **Scope of the frac_over negative: it is a geometry result, not a verdict on the coordinate.** The
  compression test varied shape and engine class at a *fixed input distribution*, so "frac_over does not
  compress without the class" is a statement about the geometry axis. It says nothing about the input
  distribution axis, where a separate measurement (§2.3, `results/a11_layernorm_reference_dist.json`) finds
  reference consumption rising near-linearly in row conditioning from rho 0.011 at kappa 1.73 to 2.529 at
  kappa 1000. So frac_over is not uncompressible in general; it is uncompressible *from geometry alone*.
- **frac_over does not compress without the class, and it is not admission.** Bulk-failure classification
  (frac_over > 0.5) is 100% out-of-fold when the class is supplied and 79% from geometry alone — and the 21%
  that fails is exactly the dispatched rows. frac_over is the reference-accuracy diagnostic that admission is
  conditioned on, not a candidate-level admission test (§4.1); the candidate-level test is measured in §4.6
  and returns a different, stronger answer.
- **The class itself is barely predictable.** A leave-one-shape-out nearest-centroid classifier over the four
  derived coordinates (log k_eff, log n_elem, log C, log H) reaches 0.604 against a 0.5625 majority baseline
  (TF32) on the 48-shape lattice; the classifier, its features, its folds and its per-row predictions are in
  `results/a8b_analysis.json` (`class_predictability`, `class_preds`). Deployment consequence, stated plainly: the certificate is
  not free. It costs one profiler pass per shape family to learn the class, and that cost belongs in the
  row rather than in a footnote. Scope of that inference, since it is easy to overread: a nearest-centroid
  failure does not establish that engine class is unpredictable *in principle* — only that these four
  coordinates do not predict it. A continuous description the classifier cannot see remains possible, and a categorical output can arise from a threshold on continuous inputs, which a nearest-centroid classifier cannot represent; the tuple test of §2.5 narrows the threshold claim to the coordinates it varied — and withdraws the stronger reading, as §2.5 states — without establishing unpredictability in any coordinate it did not vary.

**Decision rule (declared).** The compression test was pre-registered at c56c0a0 with two
questions and a frozen classifier: does rho_max compress in derived coordinates, and does
frac_over predict without the class?  The strict reading — COMPRESSION_PARTIAL — is that
magnitude compresses (LOO Spearman 0.986 with the class, 0.833 from geometry alone) while
frac_over does not (bulk 79% vs 100% out-of-fold without the class).  The continuous
reading (COMPRESSION_SUPPORTED_CONTINUOUS) is reported as measured, never as a pass: its own
prediction fails under the strict reading, and the manuscript describes it as measured,
not as shipped.  Both readings are reported; neither is hidden.

The honest summary for §4: admission is conditional on a measured categorical coordinate whose own
predictability is 0.60, and the magnitude field is predictable at 0.83 without it. A third party can use
the certificate to *evaluate* a published row without trusting us, and — §4.6 — can predict a candidate's
*admission* on an unprofiled shape from declared features at AUC 0.9747 over 672 arm-checks, matching a
two-line arm-class baseline (§4.6); what they cannot yet predict is the operating threshold inside a class,
which stays a measured per-arm quantity. That 0.9747 target is candidate admission, not the engine class: the
engine-class predictability is the separate 0.604 result above, on a different population, feature set and
label.

One retraction belongs here rather than in §2. A7 stated that 3×3 stride-1 conv routes to the winograd
engine. On this wider grid it does so only in a corner: C≥64 with small output extent (H7 at both C64 and
C256, H13 at C64), while C16 at every H and C256 at H13/H21 route to the TF32 tensorop engine. The
dispatch boundary sits between H13 and H21 at C64 and between H7 and H13 at C256, and one step across it
moves rho by 20-38×. A7's sentence was scoped to H=5 and is corrected here; the two-class decomposition
survives, but the class is a dispatch decision over (C, output extent), not a function of the conv
parameters. This is the fourth retraction recorded in this program and the second that is mine rather than
one found in review.

## 4.5c The population boundary is a plateau, and it is a separate surface from admission (s92, 2026-11-13)

s91 left the "which statistic" statement one-sided: the extremum boundary was measured, but frac_over was still
below 0.01 at the top of its sweep, so the fraction that says how much of the output is unusable had no boundary.
s92 extends the same sweep upward on the same two tuples and bisects the scale at which frac_over crosses 0.01.
Prereg committed before the run (`lab/s92_frac_boundary.py`, sha 2a4b06e1c5aa); the harness is s91's, imported
verbatim, so the tie-out is bit-level. 22 grid rows, all ok; tie-out to s91's g=64 values within 5.6e-6 and 6.5e-6

`results/s92_analysis.json`, `results/s92b_mechanism.json`.

- **The 1% population boundary is measured and it collapses across tuples.** frac_over reaches 0.01 at T1 g=76.91
  (|A|max 371.3, u=8.17) and T2 g=79.38 (|A|max 415.3, u=9.14): cross-tuple ratio 1.032 in g and 1.119 in u across
  the same 4.88x element-count difference. The frozen summary took its FRAC_BOUNDARY_PARTIAL branch (the 0.5 level
  was never reached), so the collapse clause was not evaluated by the frozen code; `s92_analysis.py` computes it
  from the committed rows and labels it post-hoc. Within the prereg's 25% bar it collapses, and the coordinate is
  statistic-specific: u*≈8-9 for the 1% population boundary against u*≈0.84-0.89 for the extremum boundary.
- **The population boundary is a plateau, not a crossing at 0.5.** frac_over rises from 0.0087 at g=64 to 0.0184 at
  g=4096 (|A|max 19,775) and 0.0183 at 21,431; the last 4x in g contributes 6.1% / 6.0% of the total rise, and rho
  in the same interval grows from 7.6 to 327 (log-log slope 0.904 / 0.924). So the extremum diverges while the
  fraction saturates: the 0.5 level does not exist in range, and the prereg's implicit expectation that frac_over
  would climb toward 1 was falsified by its own grid.
- **Mechanism measured, not asserted.** At g=4096 the evaluator predicate and a pure-relative predicate (atol=0)
  give the same failing fraction (0.01842 vs 0.01861 on T1; 0.01831 vs 0.01851 on T2), so at this scale atol is

- **Class control holds.** The tf32-off arm has frac_over exactly 0.0 and rho ≤0.012 at both crossings, and both
  crossing tuples label TF32_TENSOROP, so the population boundary is TF32-class like the extremum one.

Validity-domain update: at the shipped 1e-2 gate, on GB10 sm_120 with a default cudnn-TF32 conv reference, an exact

output, so admission and usability are separate surfaces with separate coordinates. Scoped to conv/convT at these

## 4.5d The plateau's domain: a scale-invariant, shape-independent relative-error tail (s93, 2026-11-13)

s92 measured the plateau at one scale point per tuple, which cannot separate "the predicate is relative here"
from "the relative tail is scale-invariant". s93 tests that directly, two decades above the measurement and one
decade below. Prereg `8dfa114` frozen before the run (a mechanical import-path fix, `454b5d7`, was committed before
any row existed and changed no substance); artifacts `results/s93_plateau_domain.jsonl` (10/10 ok),
`results/s93_plateau_domain_summary.json`, and the post-hoc mechanism probe `results/s93b_threshold_model.jsonl`
(8/8 ok) with `results/s93b_threshold_model_summary.json`. Controls: tie-out to s92's g=4096 rows within 1e-7
absolute on frac_over and 1.3e-6 relative on rho; A-arm repeat bit-identical 10/10; no kill clause fired
(K_FRAC_DECADE, K_TAIL_INVARIANT, K_B_FIRES all clean).

- **The relative-error distribution is exactly scale-invariant under power-of-two rescaling.** At g=16, 1024 and
  4096 the quantiles are bit-identical to the last digit: p50 2.9243e-4, p99 1.862e-2, p99.9 0.1842 on T1, and
  2.9190e-4 / 1.8524e-2 / 0.1843 on T2. That is arithmetic, not coincidence: multiplying a fp32 tensor by 2^k is
  exponent-exact, so a TF32 accumulation's relative error cannot change. At g=1e5 (not a power of two) the same
  quantiles move by 0.02% (p50 2.9259e-4, p99 1.8616e-2), consistent with weight rounding at the rescale.
- **The plateau is a measured constant and it is shape-independent within the class.** At g=1e5 frac_over is
  0.018567 (T1, 2.92M elements), 0.018492 (T2, 14.25M) and 0.018663 on a FRESH third tuple (T3: 3x3 s1 128x128
  C64->64, 1.05M elements) — a cross-tuple ratio of **1.009** over a 13.6x element-count spread, two kernel sizes
  and two output extents. All three label TF32_TENSOROP. The constant is therefore the TF32 reference's tail
  probability P(rel_err > 1e-2) = **1.85-1.87%**, not a per-shape number.
- **Below the plateau there is a measured regime, and it is the atol term doing the work.** At g=16 (|A|max
  77-84) frac_over collapses to 2.71e-4 / 2.75e-4 while the pure-relative fraction stays at 1.861% / 1.851%: 98.5%
  of the tail is absorbed by the absolute floor. The absorbed mass is 1.83e-2 at g=16, 7.9e-4 at g=1024 and
  1.1e-5 at g=1e5, i.e. it decays as the budget's atol share decays.
- **The mechanism is reconstructed exactly, not fitted.** s93b (post-hoc, declared after s93's numbers) recomputes
  the evaluator predicate as P(rel > rtol + atol/|X_e|) from the measured per-element |X_e|: agreement is **0.0
  absolute on 8/8 points** (verdict MODEL_EXACT), with the threshold at the median |X| measured at 0.010933 for
  g=16, 0.010015 for g=1024 and 0.010000 for g=1e5. The model carries no free parameter, so its value is the
  decomposition it licenses — how much of the tail the absolute floor hides at each scale — rather than a new
  discovery. What it rules out is the alternative reading that the plateau is a fitted saturation.
- **Class control.** The tf32-off arm's frac_over is ≤7.3e-5 at every point (rho ≤14.7), so the constant belongs
  to the TF32 engine class, not to conv arithmetic as such.

Validity-domain statement, replacing s92's point estimate: at the shipped 1e-2 gate on GB10 sm_120 with a default
cudnn-TF32 conv reference, the population statistic has a measured ceiling of 1.85-1.87% of outputs for any scale
where the absolute floor is negligible (|A|max ≳ 2e4, u ≳ 4e2), approached from below (1.78% at |A|max 4.9e3);
below |A|max ≈ 80 the same reference fails only 0.03% of elements because atol raises the effective relative
threshold to ~0.011. So the two statistics now have two measured domains rather than one measured point: admission
is governed by the extremum at u*≈0.87, usability by a shape-independent tail constant that the gate's own
absolute floor can suppress by a factor of 70 at small scales. Scoped to conv/convT, three tuples, one draw per
point, GB10 only.

## 4.5e The reference-induced share outside conv is not a family constant, and the depth law does not survive as a power law (s94, 2026-11-13)

Two debts are closed here. The first is the decomposition that had been deferred four ticks: whether the 0.9985
reference-induced share measured on the TF32 conv population (s84/s87) transfers to a family whose reference error
is driven by accumulation depth rather than by an engine-class constant. The second is whether that depth can be
written as a law, `eps(K)`, which is what would turn s91's class constant into a coordinate rather than a label.

Population declared by rule before the run from A6b/A6d/A6f, not selected by this run's outcome: fp32 matmul at the
benchmark's own flags (matmul tf32 off, precision highest), M=N=1024 with K in {1024, 2048, 4096, 8192}, plus a fresh
geometry M=N=512 at K=8192. Prereg frozen pre-run (`lab/s94_nonconv_decomp.py` docstring, committed before execution);
artifacts `results/s94_nonconv_decomp.jsonl` (5/5 ok), `results/s94_nonconv_decomp_summary.json`, and the post-hoc
`results/s94_analysis.json`. Instrument controls: A-arm repeat bit-identical 5/5, fp64-anchor repeat bit-identical 5/5,
no kill fired.

- **The compile candidate is bit-identical to the reference on this family (5/5).** That is the reason s73's design
  could not decompose anything here: Inductor emits the same cuBLAS call, so |C−A| = 0. The decomposition is carried
  instead by a chunked-accumulation candidate (K split into eight contiguous chunks, each a fp32 matmul, summed in
  fp32 in fixed order), declared in the prereg as a different accumulation tree and the hand-kernel analog.
- **The reference's extremum crosses the 1e-4 budget between K=2048 and K=4096.** rho_ref is 0.580 at K=1024, 0.559
  at 2048, 1.235 at 4096 and 2.449 at 8192, so the crossing is bracketed by the grid and lands in the same interval
  as A6d's bisected 3608/3632 on a different draw. The fresh geometry gives 1.652 at K=8192 (M=N=512), so the
  geometry term reproduces in direction (ratio 0.67 against the 1024-wide tuple) and the depth effect is not an
  artifact of one output extent.
- **The share does not transfer: it is 0.56, not 0.9985.** Across the three tuples with failures the decomposition
  is 300 failing elements, 168 reference-induced and 132 candidate-induced (pooled share 0.560), per-tuple 0.600 /
  0.562 / 0.541 (spread 0.059). Verdict REF_SHARE_PARTIAL. The conv constant is therefore a statement about a
  population in which the reference carries a coarse engine, not a property of reference-versus-candidate
  comparison as such. §4.5f shows this point estimate is one draw of a distribution: across sixteen fresh draws at
  K≥4096 the share spans 0.25-0.75 (mean 0.502, sd 0.134), so the partial share is quoted with its draw spread.
- **What sets the share is which arm carries the coarse engine.** The tf32-on candidate arm fails 3.50M elements at
  1e-4 with a reference-induced share of 7.0e-5, i.e. essentially all of its failure is its own. So the three
  measured cases line up as a carrier rule: reference carries TF32 → 0.9985 (conv, s84/s87); both arms fp32 with
  comparable error → 0.56 (this run); candidate carries TF32 → ~0 (this run).
- **Mechanism candidate, not a law.** The two fp32 arms have comparable extremum error (rho_cand/rho_ref 1.009 /
  1.108 / 0.956 / 0.986 / 1.149), so a crude equal-carrier model rho_cand/(rho_cand+rho_ref) predicts 0.489 / 0.496
  / 0.535 against the measured 0.600 / 0.562 / 0.541 — max deviation 0.111, i.e. the right order and the right
  insensitivity to K, but not exact. It is recorded as a mechanism candidate because it survives its own comparison
  without a fitted parameter.
- **The depth law is intermediate, not a power law.** The log-log fit of rho_ref against K over the four fixed-geometry
  points gives slope 0.738 with a maximum log residual of 0.32, and it is not even monotone at the bottom: 0.580 →
  0.559 is a −3.7% step from K=1024 to 2048, while the segment slopes are −0.054 (1024→2048), 1.144 (2048→4096) and
  0.988 (4096→8192). Verdict DEPTH_LAW_INTERMEDIATE. So the reflection's proposed replacement of a class constant by
  `eps(K)` is NOT established: the crossing location replicates, the exponent does not. What the data support is a
  piecewise statement (a noise-floor regime below K≈2048, then a near-linear rise above it), which is what §2.4
  carries rather than a single exponent. **§4.5f resolves both halves of this reading on eight fresh draws:** the
  −3.7% step appears in 1 of 8 draws and is retired as draw noise, while the per-draw exponent is stable at
  0.771 ± 0.081 and the amplitude is not.

Validity-domain statement: at the strict 1e-4 gate on GB10 sm_120, a default fp32 matmul reference exceeds the budget
at K ≥ ~3.6k (measured crossing bracketed 2048 < K < 4096, replicating A6d), and a candidate failure in that regime is
roughly half reference-induced — the split is set by which arm carries the coarse engine (measured 0.9985 / 0.56 / ~0
for reference-carried, equal-carried and candidate-carried cases). Scoped to matmul, five tuples, one draw per tuple,
fp32 only; the depth exponent is not established. §4.5f replaces the one-draw readings with eight-draw distributions
and retires the low-K floor.

## 4.5f A draw-repeat refutes the low-depth floor and shows the amplitude is not draw-stable (s95, 2026-11-13)

s94 rested on one draw per tuple, so its non-monotone step and its share point estimate were single samples. s95
repeats the fixed-geometry measurement with eight independent draws at each of K=1024/2048/4096/8192 at M=N=1024
(prereg `80a91f6` frozen pre-run; artifacts `results/s95_draw_repeat.jsonl`, 33/33 rows ok including the instrument
repeat, and `results/s95_analysis.json`). The tick's reflection named a bold conjecture before the run — that the
low-K behaviour is a real two-regime floor set by the reference's per-element rounding — and coded the branch in
advance: FLOOR_REAL only if the K=1024→2048 decrease reproduced in at least 7 of 8 draws and exceeded twice the
pooled draw standard deviation.

- **The floor conjecture is refuted.** The decrease appears in 1 of 8 draws. The means run 0.504 at K=1024 → 0.626 at
  K=2048, a 24% increase at 1.83 pooled sd. Branch FLOOR_NOISE. s94's −3.7% step was one draw landing low at K=1024,
  so the piecewise floor reading is retired rather than carried.
- **The exponent is draw-stable; the amplitude is not.** Per-draw log-log slopes over the four K values are
  0.692-0.900 (mean 0.771, sd 0.081), tighter than the 0.32 log residual of s94's single pooled fit. At fixed K,
  however, rho_ref spans 1.39x at K=1024 (0.452-0.627), 1.37x at 2048, 1.20x at 4096 and 1.53x at 8192, with CV
  12.7 / 11.1 / 6.6 / 14.3%, so the prereg's stability kill fires above 10% and no exponent claim is issued this
  tick. What the data support is a scaling law with a draw-dependent amplitude, `eps(K) = c(draw)·K^0.77`, and a
  per-tuple rho_ref is a sample from that distribution rather than a property of the tuple.
- **The reference is clean at the shipped gate on this family at every draw.** frac_over_ref at 1e-2 is exactly 0.0
  on all 32 rows; at the strict gate it is 0.0 at K≤2048, 3.6e-6 at 4096 and 1.9e-4 at 8192. The matmul reference's
  strict-gate exceedance is confined to K≥4096 and is three to four orders of magnitude below conv's 1.85% tail, so
  the conv tail constant is not a general reference property.
- **The share is draw-dependent, not a constant.** Sixteen rows at K≥4096, where the chunked candidate actually
  fails, give share 0.25-0.75 (mean 0.502, sd 0.134) → SHARE_UNSTABLE, since s94's 0.560 differs from the mean by
  0.058 against the prereg's 0.05 bar. s94's per-tuple 0.600/0.562/0.541 all lie inside that distribution, so the
  partial share ships as a distribution with its spread. At K≤2048 the chunked candidate does not exceed at all
  (rho_cand < 1), so the share has no support there.

Validity-domain statement: on GB10 sm_120, fp32 matmul at M=N=1024 with the benchmark's own flags, the reference's
extremum is a draw-distributed quantity whose log-log depth slope is stable across draws (0.77 ± 0.08) while its
amplitude varies up to 1.53x at fixed K, and the reference is clean at the shipped 1e-2 gate on every draw. Scoped to
matmul, one geometry, fp32, eight draws per K; eight draws resolve a 3.7% effect but do not characterize the tail.

## 4.5g The amplitude has no input driver, no engine driver, and no location driver: it is a rounding-magnitude lottery (s96/s96b, 2026-11-13)

§4.5f left the amplitude unexplained: at fixed geometry rho_ref varied up to 1.53x across draws and no recorded
covariate tracked it. s96 tests four named explanations at one geometry — M=N=1024, K=4096 — with 32 independent
draws, the benchmark's fp32 flags, spectral covariates of both operands, an engine-identity profiler pass on a
declared subset of eight draws, and a tail-quantile ladder (prereg `cb23e44` frozen pre-run; artifacts
`results/s96_amplitude_mechanism.jsonl` 33/33 rows ok including the instrument repeat, `results/s96_analysis.json`).

- **No predeclared covariate predicts the amplitude.** rho_ref spans 1.046-1.474 (1.41x, mean 1.226, CV 9.04%).
  sigma_max(a) spans 95.40-96.27 (CV 0.20%), the condition proxy sigma_max/sigma_min spans 2.951-3.014 (CV 0.51%),
  median |X| has CV 0.125% and max |X| CV 4.4%. Spearman against rho_ref: −0.192 (sigma_max(a)), −0.126 (kappa),
  −0.008 (median |X|), −0.217 (max |X|), −0.005 (max absolute error), +0.050 (extremum cancellation depth). Every
  predeclared arm is below the 0.40 bar, so the coded verdict is AMPLITUDE_LOTTERY: no predictor ships and the
  column carries the measured spread.
- **The predictor arms had almost no leverage, and that is a design fact worth stating.** Gaussian draws put
  sigma_max(a) inside 0.2% by the Marchenko-Pastur law, so the spectral hypothesis is not falsified in general — it
  is untested where conditioning actually varies. sigma_max(b) was recorded but not a predeclared verdict arm and
  returns −0.558; at 0.22% predictor variance that is a candidate for a designed-conditioning test, not a finding.
- **The engine is not the source.** Eight profiled draws plus the instrument repeat all show one kernel set,
  `cutlass_80_simt_sgemm_256x128_8x4_nn_align1` (plus a device memset), so ENGINE_STABLE and the engine-lottery
  explanation is rejected: the reference realization is reproducible at fixed shape.
- **The bulk tail is nearly stable while the maximum is not.** CV across the ladder is 0.75% at p99, 1.11% at
  p99.9, 2.02% at p99.99, 3.15% at p99.999, against 9.04% for the maximum itself (ratio 0.223 at p99.99). The prereg's
  tail clause required CV(rho_ref) > 0.10 and measured 0.0904, so it fires TAIL_INDETERMINATE verbatim; the ladder's
  monotone rise with tail depth is the shape extreme-value sampling predicts, and it is reported as such rather than
  promoted to a mechanism.

**s96b, declared post-hoc, locates the variance inside the ratio.** s96 recorded the argmax of the absolute error,
which is a different element from the argmax of the evaluator ratio. s96b re-runs the 32 draws and records the
ratio-argmax element (`results/s96b_ratio_argmax.jsonl`, 32/32 ok): |X| at that element spans 5.9e-4 to 0.41, i.e.
cancellation depth 1.4e-5 to 9.5e-3 of the median output magnitude, so every worst element is deep cancellation. The
identity log rho = log e − log(1e-4·(1+|X_e|)) holds to 8e-7, and inside it the absolute error spans
1.075e-4-1.738e-4 (1.62x, CV 12.8%) with Spearman(rho, e) = 0.661 while Spearman(rho, depth) = −0.021. The amplitude
is therefore an error-magnitude lottery at the worst-cancelled element, not a location lottery and not a threshold
effect: the atol term varies only 1.006-1.41x across these draws.

**Consequence for the certificate.** All 32 draws exceed rho_ref = 1 at K=4096 (minimum 1.046), so the upper end of
s94's 2048 < K < 4096 crossing bracket is draw-robust even though its magnitude is a sample. The rule this supports
is that a per-shape rho_ref is quoted with its draw distribution — not as a property of the shape — and that a
reference-clean verdict at the shipped gate is the statistic to rely on, since the population tail moves by 2% while
the extremum moves by 41%.

Validity-domain statement: on GB10 sm_120, fp32 matmul, M=N=1024, K=4096, the reference's extremum-to-budget ratio is
a draw-distributed quantity (1.05-1.47 over 32 draws) whose variance is carried by the absolute rounding error at the
worst-cancelled output element, with no dependence on operand conditioning at Gaussian inputs, no dependence on
dispatch (one cutlass SIMT kernel across nine profiled draws), and no dependence on cancellation depth. Scoped to one
geometry, fp32, 32 draws; the spectral hypothesis remains untested where sigma_max actually varies.

## 4.5h Conditioning is designed, not sampled, and the amplitude does not move: H1 retired (s99, 2026-11-14)

§4.5g's negative on spectral amplification carried an honest caveat: Gaussian draws hold sigma_max(a) inside 0.2%, so
the hypothesis was untested exactly where conditioning varies. s99 designs the conditioning instead. At M=N=1024,
K=4096 with the benchmark's fp32 flags, the right operand is constructed as b = U·diag(s)·Vᵀ with orthonormal factors
from QR of Gaussian draws, log-spaced singular values, and Frobenius norm fixed at unit-RMS scale, so kappa(b) sweeps
1, 10, 100, 1e3 and 1e4 while shape, output scale and engine are held; five independent draws per level give 25 rows
(prereg `lab/s99_prereg.md` committed at `e35d699` before any row; artifacts `results/s99_conditioning.jsonl` 25/25,
`s99_instrument.json`, `s99_conditioning_summary.json`, `s99_analysis.json`).

- **Controls all clean.** Instrument repeat bit-identical on rho_ref and frac_over; the construction check reproduces
  the prescribed singular values to 7.8e-6 relative; and every level dispatches the same kernel set,
  `cutlass_80_simt_sgemm_256x128_8x4_nn_align1`, so conditioning does not perturb dispatch and the sweep is not an
  engine test.
- **The level means do not move.** rho_ref means are 1.341, 1.301, 1.356, 1.168 and 1.208 across kappa 1→1e4, a
  1.16x spread over four decades of conditioning (sigma_max(b) 64.0 → 273.6, a 4.3x rise at fixed Frobenius norm).
  The coded verdict is **P2_RETIRE_H1**: Spearman(kappa, rho_ref) = −0.35, below the 0.40 bar, while the row-level
  span of 1.92x clears the 1.3x clause — so the amplitude varies, just not with conditioning.
- **The variance is inside the levels, not between them.** Within-level CV of rho_ref is 9.1% at kappa=1, 11.8% at
  10, 22.7% at 100, 8.8% at 1e3 and 5.1% at 1e4 (median 9.1%), against s96's 9.04% at Gaussian inputs. Fixing
  conditioning therefore does not shrink the lottery: the draw-to-draw spread is the same quantity s96 measured.
- **Descriptive, not a verdict arm.** Spearman(median |X|, rho_ref) = +0.376 is the closest of the recorded covariates
  to the bar but was not predeclared; extremum cancellation depth gives −0.105 and sigma_max(b) −0.319. The strict-gate
  population fraction stays at 3-5e-6 and the shipped 1e-2 fraction is exactly 0.0 on every row, so nothing here
  changes any admission statement.

Validity-domain statement: on GB10 sm_120, fp32 matmul, M=N=1024, K=4096, the reference's extremum-to-budget ratio is
a draw-distributed quantity whose level mean is insensitive to operand conditioning across four decades (1.17-1.36),
with a within-conditioning draw spread of 5-23%. H1 (spectral amplification) is retired on this geometry; the s96
caveat is discharged rather than carried forward. Scoped to one geometry, one family, five draws per level.


## 4.6 Candidate admission: a class baseline plus a measured within-class threshold

The result here is narrower than a predictive certificate and is stated that way. Admission is the
evaluator-exact candidate-versus-reference verdict, and on this population it is decided by the arm class plus a
measured within-class residual, not by the reference diagnostic of §4.5. The population is the 672 arm-checks of
`results/r1_conv_gated.jsonl` (56 conv shapes × 12 arms), labelled by the evaluator-exact `pass_1e4` verdict
against the recorded default reference at the declared 1e-4 contract: 128 admissible, 544 not.

**The baseline comes first.** A two-line rule that reads only the arm class — pass if the arm is
`torch` or `torch.compile`, fail if it is a hand kernel — scores **96.4286%** (TP 108 / TN 540 / FP 4 / FN 20),
which is 15.48 pp above the pooled majority of 80.95%. Comparing a model to the pooled majority therefore
understates what is already available for free; the honest comparator is this rule.

**Declared-feature model.** Linear probability model on eight declared
features only (log2 C, log2 K, log2 H·W, N, log2 bytes, is_compile, is_hand, prec_tf32 — no measured reference
feature, no
interaction), leave-one-shape-out: **AUC 0.974652, accuracy 96.4286%**, clearing the prereg's bar (AUC ≥ 0.90).
Its correct reading is narrower than an earlier draft's: the model *matches* the
arm-class rule rather than beating it, so what the model adds is a feature-level statement — the verdict is
predictable from the declared tuple and arm class alone — not extra accuracy. Admission is therefore reported as
