# Reject

This is a relevant and potentially useful GB10 study of how cuDNN dispatch and numerical reference choices affect KernelBench measurements. The retained records and the CPU recomputations support many of the reported arithmetic values (including the corrected 34-cell delivery table, the composition ratios, and the compression correlations), and the one-architecture scope is stated candidly. The central certificate claim is nevertheless not established as written because the manuscript repeatedly conflates three different outcomes that it otherwise correctly distinguishes in §4.1.

The decisive problem is the “admission does not compress” result. The abstract reports 79% versus 100% “bulk accuracy” as evidence about admission, and §4.5 calls the resulting object an admission lookup. In `a8b_analysis.json`, however, these are out-of-fold predictions of the binary label `frac_over > 0.5`. That label is the reference’s distance from an fp64 anchor; it is not the candidate-versus-reference whole-tensor `allclose` result that defines benchmark admission. No candidate admission labels are used in that compression experiment. The paper itself acknowledges this in §4.5 and §8.4 (“not itself a candidate-level admission test”), but the abstract, introduction, and the stated certificate conclusion still make the stronger admission claim. Thus the evidence supports a bounded result about predicting a reference-error diagnostic, not the conclusion that candidate admission requires an enumerated per-tuple lookup. The authors should either perform an out-of-fold prediction study on actual candidate/reference verdicts (with held-out candidates and inputs) or remove the admission-generalization claim and present the lookup as a reporting choice for measured rows.

The same conflation appears in the framing of the central question. Section 1.1 says that “a candidate’s verdict is therefore decided by which engine the reference happened to select,” and §2.3 repeats that conclusion. The measured $ρ$ only compares $R_{\mathrm{def}}$ with an fp64 anchor; it does not determine whether an arbitrary candidate agrees with $R_{\mathrm{def}}$. A candidate identical to the reference passes regardless of the reference’s fp64 error, and the scalar construction in §4.1 explicitly gives a candidate that passes while the reference has `frac_over = 1`. The data show that the engine changes the numerical denominator and can change which implementations match it; they do not show that it decides the candidate’s verdict. This is a substantive overstatement in the abstract and the opening argument, not merely terminology. The main claim needs to be reframed around reference-diagnostic and candidate-agreement effects, with the universal verdict language withdrawn.

The dispatch lookup is also supported only by a very small designed surface: 17 cells at one base geometry, two repeats in one process on an idle device. That establishes repeat consistency under those conditions, while the manuscript’s wording (“class is a function of the declared tuple” and “deterministic partition”) suggests a stronger runtime property. The limitations mention contention and library changes, but the stronger premise remains the basis for the proposed admission lookup. Either provide replication across processes and relevant runtime conditions or state this strictly as two-repeat consistency on this build and shape lattice.

Finally, the reported deployed reduction cost of +3.53% (SE 1.37) is a kernel-time ratio that excludes the validation cost required by the D1 policy. Section 6.5 says that D1 performs a reference build, executes each checked arm, and compares outputs, while the reported ratio excludes all of that cost. Calling it the cost of the “deployed policy” therefore overstates what was measured; it is a post-validation execution regret with fallback substitution. The abstract should not present it as total deployment cost unless the checks and any compilation or fresh-input certification cost are charged. The 9/12 rate is likewise conditional on the recorded input instances, as the paper notes, and does not certify unseen inputs for a zero-probe dispatcher.

The novelty is appropriately narrow relative to KernelBench, DietCode, Ansor, Winograd error analysis, and PyTorch’s numerical-accuracy documentation; the supplied same-problem index is empty, so I found no local precedence conflict. The GB10 dispatch measurements, the explicit counterexample to worst-stage propagation, and the corrected per-row records could form a publishable bounded measurement paper. Acceptance requires first separating reference accuracy from candidate admission throughout the abstract and argument, and then either supplying the missing candidate-level generalization evidence or withdrawing that central claim.
